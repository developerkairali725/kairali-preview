## 3 · AEO answer-capsule plan (the definitional query family)

### 3.1 Why AEO is the primary strategy, not a bolt-on

- **51% of healthcare searches trigger an AI Overview** — double the all-industry average, the highest of 15 industries studied; informational intent triggers them 66.9% of the time [S11]. whatisayurveda.com's queries will almost always be answered above the organic results — the site must be built to **win the citation, not just the ranking**.
- **Long-tail question phrasing is the trigger zone:** 7+ word queries trigger AIOs 73.9% vs 35.8% for 1–2 words [S11] — architecture is built around full-sentence question clusters ("what are the three doshas in ayurveda", "is panchakarma safe for beginners"), not head terms.
- **Ranking top-10 is no longer required for citations:** only 38% of AIO-cited pages rank top-10 (down from 76% mid-2025); 26.2% of citations come from positions 11–100 and 36.7% from outside the top 100. Query fan-out decomposes "what is ayurveda" into many sub-queries and cites across all of them — **a deep topic cluster can earn citations before the domain builds head-term rankings**, the single biggest opening for a domain registered 2026-07-07 [S12, S28].

### 3.2 The capsule spec (binding on every template)

1. **Every page and every H2 section opens with a 40–60 word direct answer** to the exact question, before any elaboration — "a tight 40-60 word definition immediately beneath a heading is the gold standard for informational queries" [S13].
2. **Each capsule is followed by extraction-friendly structure** — bullets, numbered lists, short paragraphs, tables, Q&A formatting — the healthcare-specific citation factors [S11].
3. **Question-form H2s** throughout (the NCCIH pattern that both Google and the AI layer already reward) [S4].
4. **FAQPage/MedicalWebPage schema** wired to the capsules (§2.3) [S11, S14].

## 5 · CLAIMS GUARDRAILS — the educational register (copy law for this site)

**Register:** educational/group-corporate. Describe the tradition and the evidence honestly; NEVER promise to cure, treat, or prevent disease. This is simultaneously (a) the law, (b) what Google rewards for this YMYL query, and (c) what every ranking incumbent already does — Healthline states doshas lack scientific evidence on a page updated June 2025 that still ranks; ayurveda.com states Ayurveda "is not a substitute for Western allopathic medicine" [S5, S7].

1. **DMR Act 1954 (India, criminal):** advertising cures for listed diseases is prohibited; Schedule J conditions (diabetes, cancer, obesity, mental illness, alopecia, leucoderma etc.) can never be linked to treatment/product claims. Banned phrasings: "100% safe", "free from side effects", "guaranteed treatment", "permanent cure". ASCI actively enforces digital content — 233 health ads reported to the AYUSH Ministry for DMR violations in 2024–25 [S19].
2. **The promotional trip-wire:** the moment an educational page links to a KAPPL product or KTAHV programme, it becomes promotional in the regulator's eyes — **definitional pages stay claim-free; commercial intent routes via clearly separated CTA modules (§7), never via in-copy efficacy language** [S19].
3. **The .org anti-pattern list is the ban list:** "cure for slipped disc", "permanent relief from arthritis", "control your diabetes", hypertension/migraine/bronchial/heart/depression treatment claims, "breast enhancements", "skin fairness" — all found live on the .org and all stripped [S27].
4. **YMYL disclaimers:** alternative-medicine sites are explicitly YMYL and held to the higher-harm standard [S15]. Every health page carries a medical disclaimer + "complement, not replacement" framing + see-a-qualified-physician guidance — the register of every incumbent [S2, S4].
5. **Evidence language is hedged, NCCIH-style:** "a few studies suggest", "may help", studies "small" or "not well-designed" — with prominent safety data [S4]. Traditional-use framing is descriptive/cultural ("described in the Charaka Samhita as…"), never a present-day benefit promise.
6. **Register boundaries within the site:** KTAHV references use the clinical lock (patient, physician, treatment, programme; never spa/resort/luxury/guests — the .org's spa ×35 / resort ×22 / luxury ×4 never carries over) [S27, CLAUDE.md]. Villa Raag references may use wellness-hospitality register. Do not cross-apply.
7. **Locales:** EN first; DE/FR per site brief — any page shipping EN-only is explicitly flagged (CLAUDE.md locked facts). Banned-sense equivalents (Kur/Urlaub/Luxus; cure/vacances/luxe) checked wherever KTAHV is referenced.
8. **Enforcement hook:** clinical/medical content → Dr. Deepu John sign-off (CLAUDE.md rule 7); any proposed claim that names a condition → Abilash/legal before publish (ESCALATION rule); compliance-lint candidate for CI.
