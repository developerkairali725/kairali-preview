**Understanding Ayurveda**

## **The Meaning of Ayurveda**

| :-: |
| \*\*\*"Ayusho Veda: Ayurveda"\*\*\*Ayur means Life  ·  Veda means Knowledge\*In essence, Ayurveda is 'the knowledge — or science — of life.'\* |

## **Understanding Ayurveda in Brief**

Famous for its restorative therapies and natural medicines, Ayurveda remains an integral part of India's philosophy of holistic living and continues to play a significant role in healthy living today. Rooted in the principles of the Atharvaveda, Ayurveda seeks the complete restoration of health: it treats every ailment at its root cause while also relieving the immediate symptoms.

According to Ayurveda, health is the balanced state of the **Doshas** (the three bio-energies: Vata, Pitta and Kapha), **Agni** (the digestive fires), the **Dhatus** (the seven bodily tissues) and the **Malas** (the body's excretory products) — together with a contented state of the soul, senses and mind. The aim of this holistic approach is two-fold: to treat the body, and to restore harmony between mind, body and spirit. It is this completeness that makes Ayurveda an effective and enduring wellness science.

## **The Origin of Ayurveda**

The origins of Ayurveda are woven through history and tradition. It is held that the knowledge was passed down from Lord Brahma through a lineage of sages, and in time brought to humankind for a healthy and happy existence. In its recorded form, Ayurveda is preserved in the classical texts: the *Charaka Samhita, Sushruta Samhita and Ashtanga Hridaya* — together known as the **Brihatrayee** (the Great Triad) — and the *Sharangdhara Samhita, Bhavaprakasha and Madhava Nidana* — known as the **Laghutrayee** (the Lesser Triad). Over the centuries these works have been translated into many languages, carrying Ayurveda to the world.

## **The Eight Branches of Ayurveda**

*Classical Ayurveda is organised into eight branches, together known as Ashtanga Ayurveda.*

  - **Kaya Chikitsa (General Medicine)** — Comparable to general internal medicine, this branch treats disorders of the whole body and its systems — respiratory, digestive, nervous and more — including conditions such as asthma, bronchitis, indigestion, fever, haemorrhoids and rheumatic pain.
  - **Bala Chikitsa / Kaumarabhritya (Paediatrics)** — The nourishment, growth and care of children.
  - **Graha Chikitsa (Psychiatry)** — Ayurvedic psychology, addressing psychosomatic and mental conditions through a combination of diet, yoga, meditation and Mantra therapy.
  - **Shalakya Chikitsa (Ophthalmology & ENT)** — Diseases of the eye, ear, nose, throat and head. Acharya Nimi is regarded as the father of Shalakya Tantra.
  - **Shalya Chikitsa (Surgery)** — The management of conditions requiring surgical or para-surgical care and the removal of foreign bodies. Sushruta is revered as the father of surgery.
  - **Visha Chikitsa / Agada Tantra (Toxicology)** — The study of natural and artificial toxins and their antidotes, including the treatment of bites and stings.
  - **Jara Chikitsa / Rasayana (Geriatrics & Rejuvenation)** — The science of rejuvenation, using Rasayana preparations valued for their restorative, anti-ageing and anti-stress properties.
  - **Vrishya Chikitsa / Vajikarana (Reproductive Health)** — The care of male and female reproductive health and vitality.

## **Diagnosis in Ayurveda**

Where modern medicine often addresses symptoms, Ayurveda looks to the equilibrium of the doshas — the body's fundamental energy cycles. Any disease is understood as a manifestation of imbalance among these energies, brought on by internal and external factors.

Ayurveda holds that our physical and mental functioning is governed by three doshas — Vata, Pitta and Kapha — themselves combinations of the five elements: Ether, Air, Fire, Water and Earth. When the doshas are in balance, a person enjoys calm and well-being; when disturbed — by seasonal change, sedentary lifestyle, ageing or hormonal shifts — a range of health issues can follow. In harmony, the doshas support a serene state of the *Atma* (soul), the *Indriyas* (senses) and the *Manas* (mind).

## **Dosha, Dhatu & Mala**

|  |
| :-: |
| \*\*\*"Dosha-Dhatu-Mala Moolam Hi Sareeram"\*\*\*The body is founded upon the three Doshas, seven Dhatus and three Malas. |

**Dosha** — the energy that regulates our physical and psychological processes. Doshas are present in every living thing, from the simplest cell to the most complex organism; imbalance among them gives rise to ailment and disease.

**Dhatu** — one of the body's structural tissues. In balance, the Dhatus support the growth and sustenance of the body.

**Mala** — one of the body's waste products. The Malas support the body's natural elimination, ensuring wastes are continually cleared and kept within healthy limits.

## **The Tridosha — Vata, Pitta & Kapha**

*The three doshas govern the entire physiology of the body — the way you respond to the world around you.*

### **Vata**

**Function:** governs movement and underlies all bodily and mental processes.

**When imbalanced:** a raised Vata can show as discolouration of the skin, a weakened abdomen, pain and insomnia; a lowered Vata, as reduced activity and slow reflexes.

### **Pitta**

**Function:** governs digestion, body temperature, vision, appetite, intellect and the health of the skin.

**When imbalanced:** a raised Pitta can show as excessive hunger and thirst, a burning sensation and disturbed sleep; a lowered Pitta, as poor digestion, cold extremities and dull skin.

### **Kapha**

**Function:** governs stability, lubrication and the firmness of the joints.

**When imbalanced:** a raised Kapha can show as sluggish digestion, heaviness, excess sleep and cough; a lowered Kapha, as giddiness, palpitations, dryness of the skin and looseness of the joints.

## **Prakruti — Your Unique Constitution**

**Prakruti** is your individual constitution — the genetic, physical and mental make-up that gives each person their unique character. It is set at conception and shapes not only temperament but also physical nature and susceptibility to particular conditions. Understanding a person's Prakruti is therefore essential to diagnosing and treating illness — someone of predominantly Vata constitution, for example, may be more prone to joint complaints such as arthritis.

Ayurveda groups Prakruti into seven broad types, formed from one or more doshas:

  - Vata
  - Pitta
  - Kapha
  - Vata–Pitta
  - Pitta–Kapha
  - Vata–Kapha
  - Tridosha (a balance of all three)

In practice, a single-dosha constitution is rare; most people are a combination of two doshas, known as *Dwidoshaja Prakruti*.

### **In brief — the three constitutional types**

**Vata constitution:** typically a lean build, with dry skin and hair, a variable appetite and a tendency to feel the cold; often imaginative and sensitive, with lighter sleep.

**Pitta constitution:** typically a medium build, with smooth skin, a strong appetite and a sharp, clear memory; intolerant of heat, and often determined and quick-thinking.

**Kapha constitution:** typically a solid, well-built frame with a tendency to gain weight, oily skin and strong hair; calm, steady and good-natured, with sound, restful sleep.

## **Treatment in Ayurveda**

*At Kairali, every condition is addressed through one or a combination of three approaches.*

**Swastha Chikitsa** — preventive care for the healthy, designed to strengthen immunity and metabolism through therapy, diet and exercise. It is delivered chiefly through Rasayana (rejuvenation) and Vajikarana therapies.

**Shamana Chikitsa** — palliative care that calms a condition, typically for milder ailments. It is given through internal medicines such as tablets and *kashayams* (herbal decoctions), in two stages: *Hetu Vipareetam*, which addresses the immediate cause, and *Vyadhi Vipareetam*, a holistic, step-by-step recovery that begins and ends with a complete assessment of the doshas.

**Shodhana Chikitsa** — a complete, purificatory treatment that works on both the disease and its root cause, clearing the underlying imbalance and greatly reducing the chance of recurrence by strengthening the Doshas, Dhatus and Malas. Its finest expression is Panchakarma.

## **Panchakarma — The Five Cleansing Therapies**

Among the oldest treatments in Ayurveda and described in detail in the *Charaka Samhita*, Panchakarma is a complete purification of the body using specially prepared herbal oils and medicines. Through five natural cleansing methods that support the body's own elimination, it restores balance among Vata, Pitta and Kapha and renews both body and mind. At Kairali, Panchakarma is always preceded by preparatory therapies and guided throughout by our physicians.

  - **Vamanam (Therapeutic Emesis)** — clears excess Kapha and accumulated mucus; helpful for respiratory congestion and Kapha conditions such as psoriasis.
  - **Virechanam (Purgation)** — gentle, medicated cleansing of the intestines; indicated for Pitta-related conditions.
  - **Asthapana / Niruham Vasti (Decoction Enema)** — a medicated decoction enema for Vata conditions, especially of the colon, and helpful for back pain, sciatica and joint complaints.
  - **Anuvasana Vasti (Oil Enema)** — a medicated oil enema for Vata conditions such as arthritis and neuromuscular complaints, and supportive in constipation.
  - **Nasyam (Nasal Medication)** — medicated oils administered through the nasal passages to clear the channels of the head and neck; indicated for sinusitis, migraine and chronic cold.

**PART TWO**

**Our Ayurvedic Therapies**

*These therapies are the building blocks of our programmes. Your physician will select and combine those best suited to your constitution and goals. They are grouped here by family to make them easy to explore.*

## **Whole-Body Oil & Massage Therapies**

  - **Abhyangam** — A synchronised full-body massage with warm medicated oils, performed by two therapists on the traditional wooden Droni. Often the first step in any programme, it improves circulation, sleep, vitality and skin health, and helps the body adjust to the seasons.
  - **Pizhichil** — One of Kairali's signature rejuvenating therapies. Warm medicated oil is streamed continuously over the body and gently massaged in — traditionally valued for rheumatic complaints, joint pain and general revitalisation.
  - **Udwarthanam** — An invigorating massage with dry herbal powders that exfoliates and tones the skin, stimulates circulation and supports the reduction of body fat.
  - **Udgharshanam** — Similar to Udwarthanam but using a moist herbal paste; supports the breakdown of cellulite, strengthens the muscles and is helpful for some skin conditions.

## **Shiro (Head) & Dhara Therapies**

  - **Sirodhara** — A continuous, rhythmic stream of warm medicated oil poured over the forehead while the therapist works the pressure points. Deeply calming — traditionally valued for stress, sleep and mental clarity.
  - **Takradhara** — A Dhara therapy using medicated buttermilk, valued for stress, anxiety, scalp and skin complaints, and restful sleep.
  - **Dhanyamala Dhara** — A warming, full-body stream of fermented herbal liquid, traditionally used for stiffness, aches and heaviness.
  - **Thalapotichil** — A cooling herbal paste applied across the scalp beneath a cloth wrap, traditionally used for sleeplessness, stress, and hair and scalp health.
  - **Sirovasti** — Warm medicated oil retained on the head within a fitted cap for a set time — a classical therapy for the nervous system, traditionally used for migraine, facial complaints and chronic conditions of the head.
  - **Thalam** — A medicated paste held at the crown of the head, traditionally used for sleep, headache and conditions of the head and senses.

## **Kizhi (Herbal Poultice) Therapies**

*Kizhi (pronounced 'kee-zhi') are warm herbal poultices applied across the body. Different fillings suit different needs.*

  - **Elakizhi (Patra Pinda Sweda)** — Poultices of medicinal leaves warmed in oil; supports circulation and eases stiffness while cleansing and toning the skin.
  - **Podikizhi** — Poultices of herbal powders, valued for muscular spasm and stiffness of the muscles and joints.
  - **Manjalkizhi** — Poultices of turmeric and herbs, traditionally used to ease inflammation and joint discomfort.
  - **Narangakizhi** — Poultices of lemon, herbs and oil, traditionally used for stiffness, frozen shoulder and various aches and pains.
  - **Muttakizhi** — Poultices prepared with egg, traditionally used for muscle weakness and certain neuromuscular complaints.
  - **Manalkizhi** — Warmed poultices of clean sand and rock salt, traditionally used for stiffness, swelling and rheumatic discomfort.
  - **Kadikizhi** — A steamed herbal poultice therapy, traditionally used for stiffness and weight concerns.
  - **Navarakizhi** — Poultices of Navara rice cooked in herbal milk — nourishing to the muscles and skin and prized as a rejuvenating therapy.

## **Vasti (Localised Oil-Pooling) Therapies**

*In these therapies, warm medicated oil is pooled within a dam of herbal dough over a specific area for a set time, or administered as a medicated enema.*

  - **Kativasti** — For the lower back; traditionally used for stiffness, disc complaints and sciatica.
  - **Greevavasti** — For the neck and cervical region; traditionally used for cervical stiffness, spasm and desk-related strain.
  - **Januvasti** — For the knees; traditionally used for osteoarthritis and knee complaints.
  - **Urovasti** — For the chest; traditionally used to support the heart and lungs and ease respiratory discomfort.
  - **Matravasti** — A gentle medicated-oil enema to pacify Vata; suitable across all ages and helpful for back discomfort.
  - **Kashayavasti** — A medicated decoction enema and a core Panchakarma procedure; traditionally used for neuromuscular conditions.

## **Eye, ENT, Oral & Facial Care**

  - **Netradhara** — A cleansing eye-wash with medicated liquids; soothing for tired, strained eyes.
  - **Netratharpanam** — Medicated ghee pooled around the eyes within a herbal dam — a nourishing therapy traditionally used to strengthen the eyes and support vision.
  - **Karnapoornam** — Warm medicated drops retained in the ear; traditionally used for ear health and related complaints.
  - **Nasyam** — Medicated oils or powders administered through the nasal passages, with massage of the head, neck and shoulders; traditionally used for sinusitis, migraine and chronic cold.
  - **Dhoompanam** — Therapeutic inhalation of medicated smoke — a cleansing step that often concludes Nasyam.
  - **Kavalagraham & Gandoosham** — Therapeutic oil-gargling and oil-holding for oral health; traditionally used to strengthen the teeth and gums and freshen the mouth.
  - **Mukhalepam** — A herbal face pack that cleanses, brightens and rejuvenates the skin.
  - **Lepam** — Medicated herbal packs applied to a specific area to relieve discomfort or restore the skin's glow.
