2.5 Diagnostic Methodology — Ashtavidha Pariksha (v1.2 finding)

|  |
| :-: |
| Material competitive intelligence finding from PDF deep-extraction: Nattika's Pre-Arrival Checklist explicitly states: "At Nattika, the diagnosis is done using AshtasthanaPareeksha (Eight step Diagnosis) and not by NadiPariksha (Pulse diagnosis)." This is a deliberate, named, publicly disclosed clinical-protocol differentiation. |

What Ashtavidha Pariksha is

Ashtavidha Pariksha (अष्टविध परीक्षा — "eight-fold examination") is the classical Charaka- and Vagbhata-tradition systematic diagnostic protocol used in formal Ayurveda clinical practice. It assesses eight parameters in defined sequence:

Nadi (नाडी) — pulse

Mutra (मूत्र) — urine

Mala (मल) — stool

Jihva (जिह्वा) — tongue

Shabda (शब्द) — voice / speech

Sparsha (स्पर्श) — skin / touch

Drik (दृक्) — eyes

Akriti (आकृति) — overall body form / habitus

Why this disclosure matters competitively

Most consumer-facing wellness-tourism Ayurveda properties either use Nadi Pariksha alone (a popular but lighter-touch single-parameter diagnostic) or do not formally name their diagnostic protocol at all

Nattika is publicly committing to the more rigorous, classical 8-fold protocol — and explicitly differentiating from Nadi-only practice in its arrival material

This pairs with the NABH Panchakarma scope (see Section 5) to position Nattika as clinically formal, not spa-formatted Ayurveda

It also pairs with the 12-day pre-arrival dietary protocol (see Section 9.2) — the diagnostic and the preparation are framed as a single clinical pathway

|  |
| :-: |
| Strategic implication for Kairali: KTAHV almost certainly already practices Ashtavidha Pariksha — it is the standard for any NABH-accredited Ayurvedic practice — but does not currently disclose this on consumer collateral. Nattika is winning a credibility framing battle that Kairali could match with one paragraph of clinical-protocol disclosure on a public landing page or pre-arrival kit. Specifically: (1) name the diagnostic protocol Kairali physicians use; (2) explicitly frame the difference from Nadi-only practice; (3) extend to a published 12-day pre-arrival dietary preparation protocol (Section 9.2 Nattika finding). All three are zero-cost positioning moves that immediately neutralise Nattika's clinical-formality differentiation. |
