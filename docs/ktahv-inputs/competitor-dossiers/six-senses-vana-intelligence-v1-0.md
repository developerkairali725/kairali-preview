Executive thesis — one paragraph

Six Senses Vana is a 21-acre Sal-forest wellness retreat near Dehradun, Uttarakhand, that opened in January 2014 under founder Veer Singh and transitioned to operate under the Six Senses brand on 1 January 2023, becoming Six Senses' first dedicated wellness retreat globally. Property-level ownership remains with Veer Singh; Six Senses (owned by IHG Hotels & Resorts since 2019) operates under a management / brand agreement, with the original team and original modality stack — Ayurveda, Yoga, Traditional Chinese Medicine, and Tibetan Sowa Rigpa medicine — substantially retained. The retreat now sits in a three-layer brand structure (property → Six Senses brand → IHG corporate) that is unique in the locked cluster. Pricing at the entry programme is approximately USD 1,153 per night single / USD 1,988 per night double for the 14-night Ayurveda programme, placing Six Senses Vana above Ananda's Foundation rate (USD 925–2,485 single across categories) at the comparable entry point — Six Senses Vana is the locked cluster's highest-priced property at the headline programme rate, though Ananda's villa rates (up to USD 4,590) still top the cluster ceiling. Two structural moats are now confirmed cluster-unique to Six Senses Vana at snapshot: (1) operation under a global luxury hotel brand (Six Senses) with IHG One Rewards loyalty integration; and (2) formally retained Sowa Rigpa (Tibetan medicine) as one of four named pillars, with a Men-Tsee-Khang / Chagpori-trained specialist on staff continuously since 2016. For Kairali, Six Senses Vana is the cluster's single most informative reference point for the question 'should a Kairali property — Villa Raag or a Soul Forest anchor — operate under a global luxury hotel brand?' The Vana transition demonstrates that such a model is feasible without surrendering asset ownership; whether it is desirable for Kairali is the strategic question this dossier exists to inform.

What Six Senses Vana resets in the cluster moat-table

Of the 17 lanes the v2.3 Cluster Addendum and post-Ananda / post-Tulah moat-table tracked, Six Senses Vana closes, opens, or materially shifts eight in a single lock. The four most consequential: (1) global luxury hotel brand operation — was 0 of 14 before this lock; now 1 of 15, cluster-unique to Six Senses Vana. (2) IHG One Rewards loyalty integration — was 0 of 14; now 1 of 15, cluster-unique. The IHG One Rewards points-earning and redemption capability on Six Senses Vana stays is a structural distribution advantage no other locked cluster property holds. (3) Sowa Rigpa / Tibetan medicine modality — was 0 of 14 in cluster-wide locked properties (Ananda offers some Tibetan-influenced treatments but does not formally offer Sowa Rigpa in its complete avatar); now 1 of 15, with Six Senses Vana the only locked property explicitly offering Sowa Rigpa as a formal medical system. (4) Uttarakhand geographic concentration — was 1 of 14 (Ananda alone); now 2 of 15. This creates the cluster's first regional sub-cluster: two ultra-luxury wellness retreats roughly 70–80 km apart by road, in the same state, targeting overlapping international wellness markets. The other four resets — CN Traveller Wellness & Spa Awards lane carry-forward, multi-modality clinical breadth lane, Six Senses brand sustainability standards as a sustainability proxy, and TCM-as-formal-modality lane — are detailed in Section 9.4.

1.6 Family, child & accessibility policies — SS-era policy evolution

V-era Vana operated as an explicitly adult-wellness sanctuary — no children permitted, 7-night minimum stay, strict phone restrictions. SS-era Vana has materially evolved on two of these three policy axes while retaining the third:

• Children: "Grow With Six Senses" programme introduced 2023 explicitly welcomes children aged 6 and above, with art, music, movement, storytelling, and interactive learning spaces designed for children. However, Booking.com still surfaces a "Children not allowed" policy on standard wellness retreat bookings at snapshot, suggesting children may be welcomed only via specifically designated family programmes / dates rather than as standard policy. CN Traveller India's Aug-Oct 2025 "Peace Makers" feature confirms "Five-night stays and children are welcomed" — but the operational reality appears to be: children admitted on specific programmes only, not as standard. This is a policy ambiguity worth verifying with the property directly before quoting to family-buying agents.

• Minimum stay: Reduced from 7 nights (V-era) to 5 nights (SS-era). This is materially shorter than Ananda's 5-night Foundation / 7-night Comprehensive minima (which are roughly comparable, in fact). The 14-night minimum applies specifically to the Ayurveda programme.

• Phone / photography: Strict restriction retained. Phones not permitted in shared / outdoor spaces, only in guestrooms or in the new Sketchbook shared working space (which Six Senses introduced specifically to accommodate guests with unavoidable work obligations). This is the single most visible "old Vana" continuity element under Six Senses operations — and a meaningful differentiator from most luxury resorts.

• Pets: Booking.com states "Pets are allowed. Charges may apply." This is unusual for a wellness retreat of this category and stands as a differentiator from Ananda (children under 14 not welcomed; pet policy not prominently featured). However, given the wellness positioning, on-property pet hosting is likely subject to programme type and specific dates.
