# VILLA RAAG — Guest Buyer Journey

*Discovery → Stay → Advocacy · a detailed, delegation-ready journey map*

Kairali Ayurvedic Group · Agonda Beach, South Goa · An ultra-private, six-room beachfront wellness retreat

**Version 1.0 · June 2026 · Confidential — Internal Use Only**

## 1. How to use this document

This is the master guest journey for Villa Raag. It is synthesised from the competitive-set audit, the best-in-class journeys of the global wellness benchmarks (Kamalaya, Ananda in the Himalayas, Fivelements, Santani), and the review-driven priorities drawn from what guests at those properties actually single out.

Every touchpoint is tagged with an owning role and a "what good looks like" standard, so the journey can be decomposed into micro action-points and delegated. Read it in this order: Sections 2–4 first (positioning and guardrails, who we serve, delivery team); Section 5 (the journey stage by stage); Sections 6–8 (program overlays, signature-experiences library, cross-cutting systems/standards/risk map); Section 9 (decomposition into micro action-points and a delegation tracker).

## 2. Positioning & non-negotiable guardrails

### 2.1 What Villa Raag is

Villa Raag is a six-room, roughly two-acre, exclusive-use beachfront wellness retreat on Agonda Beach in South Goa, offered all-inclusive from ₹45,000 plus taxes per night. It delivers personalised, one-on-one yoga and wellness — no group-class format, no fixed routines — with butler and concierge service, a temperature-controlled saltwater pool, a Zen garden, beachfront and rooftop yoga spaces, and a gourmet kitchen. It draws on the 118-year Kairali Ayurveda lineage, but is positioned as a standalone identity, with the Group association used as a secondary trust signal, not the lead.

### 2.2 The positioning line (use this everywhere)

**Villa Raag is wellness lifestyle — not Ayurvedic medicine.** Yoga, meditation, breathwork, sattvic and coastal-wellness cuisine, and mindful living — inspired by Ayurveda, never delivered as clinical Ayurvedic treatment. Any reference to Ayurveda is "in the spirit of" / "inspired by" / "complementary to."

### 2.3 Compliance guardrails (hard rules)

- **No clinical or medical claims at Villa Raag.** Do not use "treatment of [condition]," "cure," "therapy for [disease]," "doctor-led," "Panchakarma," or "clinical." Use "supports," "restorative," "inspired by," "in the spirit of," "wellness."
- **Why.** The Drugs & Magic Remedies (Objectionable Advertisements) Act 1954 applies to advertising even for non-clinical properties, and Villa Raag has no resident BAMS physician by design.
- **Clinical demand is a referral, not a build.** A guest who needs genuine clinical Ayurveda is a warm cross-sell to KTAHV (Palakkad) — not an on-site service. Keep the two brands' language separate: KTAHV uses clinical/healthcare framing for its own position; Villa Raag must not.
- **Police the launch copy.** Some press coverage has described Villa Raag as offering "clinical-grade Ayurvedic programming." Correct this wording wherever it appears.

### 2.4 The five review-proven levers

- **Personalised food with chef engagement** — the single biggest review-driver at every property.
- **Named individuals who care** — guests attribute transformation to people (a chef, a teacher, a guide), never to facilities.
- **One signature ritual** — the ceremony or flower-bath that becomes the memory they photograph and retell.
- **Arrival micro-moments and small repeated touches** — they decide first impressions and word-of-mouth.
- **Unhurried, genuine attention** — full session times, with information flowing across the whole team.

### 2.5 The risk map (design these out)

- **Acoustic privacy between the six rooms** — the single most common complaint at the closest-scale benchmark. One loud neighbour can ruin a stay. Test before peak season.
- **Service consistency** — a guest should never "have to ask for everything." Anticipate.
- **Treatment pacing** — never stack long back-to-back treatments; guests elsewhere came back "in pain."
- **Up-selling pressure** — it shows up in reviews instantly and corrodes the luxury feel.
- **The food promise is sacred** — never let it break under logistics stress (late arrival, dietary restriction).

## 4. The delivery team & roles

### 4.1 Staffing verdict: how many yoga teachers?

**One yoga teacher cannot run the program. You need two on the yoga side — plus a separate therapist and the chef.** The "program" is multi-discipline: movement + bodywork + personalised cuisine + curation + the signature ceremony. A single teacher fails on three counts — (1) at full villa, the morning one-on-one session math does not fit one person; (2) it is a seven-day operation, so one person means no coverage for days off, sickness or leave — a single point of failure for the entire premium positioning; (3) no one teacher excels across dynamic, therapeutic, breathwork and ceremony.

Minimum viable wellness team at six rooms: 1 Lead Wellness Curator (senior yoga teacher) + 1 supporting yoga / movement teacher + 1 spa / bodywork therapist (scale to 2 at full occupancy) + the chef. The second teacher and the therapist can be seconded or rotated from KTAHV's bench rather than cold-hired — capital-light coverage.

Launch-lean is acceptable only while occupancy is 1–3 rooms, on one exceptional teacher-curator with an on-call freelance second and a rotated KTAHV therapist for bodywork. Add the permanent second teacher before marketing the programs hard — the wellness layer is currently absent from Villa Raag's reviews, and that is the gap to close.

## 5. The journey, stage by stage

Eight stages, from first discovery to post-stay advocacy.

### Stage 1 — Discovery & Consideration (pre-booking)

**Purpose:** turn a searcher into an enquiry by promising a transformative, ultra-private coastal reset for a handful of guests — and by framing each program as a journey with an outcome, not a package.

| Touchpoint | What happens | Owner | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Channels (web, OTA, Instagram, B2B agents) | One consistent promise across every channel; programs presented as named journeys with simple outcomes. | Marketing | Same core promise everywhere; outcome language ("sleep deeper," "move with ease," "think clearer," "feel lighter"); non-clinical throughout. |
| "Your Journey at Villa Raag" page | Day 0 to departure described — welcome ritual, daily rhythm, signature ceremony, departure. | Marketing | Live on the site; condensed into OTA descriptions so even OTA guests feel they're entering a curated experience. |
| Social proof | Named practitioners with faces and bios; real on-site photography; guest words curated by journey stage. | Marketing | No stock imagery; named people, never "our team of wellness experts." |
| Enquiry handling | Fast, human, consultative first response that asks about the guest's goal. | Enquiry desk / Curator | First response within a defined SLA, opening with a question about the goal — not a price dump. |

**Signature moment:** the program-as-journey framing — an outcome story, not a list of inclusions.

### Stage 2 — Booking & Confirmation (T-0, within 2 hours)

| Touchpoint | What happens | Owner | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmation + warm welcome | A personal confirmation that names the assigned Curator. | EA (Ritu) / Concierge | Sent within 2 hours; warm and specific, not a system receipt. |
| Curator assigned | One named owner attached to the guest from this moment to T+28. | GM / Curator | Single point of contact established immediately. |
| Arrival Concierge / Intake form | Captures sleep, stress, mobility, energy/digestion, food preferences and restrictions, sunrise/sunset preference, the goal, and the occasion. | Curator | Feeds program selection and the chef brief; never re-asked later. |

**Signature moment:** the named Curator introduces themselves personally (WhatsApp voice note) within 2 hours of booking.

### Stage 3 — Pre-Arrival (T-7 to T-1)

| Touchpoint | What happens | Owner | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intake review + program confirmation | Curator reviews the intake, confirms or recommends the program, flags any clinical need. | Curator | Program locked; any clinical requirement routed to KTAHV as a referral. |
| Pre-arrival lifestyle & diet primer | A light 7-day primer to prime results (sleep, hydration, reducing stimulants). | Curator | Framed as wellness guidance, never prescription. |
| Welcome pack (PDF / WhatsApp) | Property map, a sample day, packing list, transfer details, what to expect. | Concierge | Sent 3–5 days out; personalised, not generic. |
| Chef brief | Dietary needs, program and preferences logged before arrival. | Curator → Chef | Chef plans the guest's personalised menus in advance. |
| Logistics | Transfer arranged (cool car + welcome water), arrival time confirmed, room readied with personalised touches. | Concierge | Zero friction on arrival day. |

**Signature moment:** the personalised welcome pack plus a short voice note from the Curator.

### Stage 4 — Arrival & Welcome (Day 0)

**Purpose:** the first sixty minutes decide the review.

| Touchpoint | What happens | Owner | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transfer experience | Car cool and running, chilled welcome water, a gracious driver. | Concierge / driver | The "two small details" that signal the guest chose right. |
| Arrival ritual | A cool herbal towel, a signature welcome tonic, and a small symbolic gift (a shell or tulsi token). | Butler | Unhurried; a calming cue — "you can slow down now, we'll handle the rest." |
| In-room check-in | Paperwork pre-done or completed seated, in the room. | Butler | Never a lobby counter or queue. |
| Orientation | Daily rhythm, the pool / rooftop / beach spaces, beach-walk routes, the WhatsApp channel. | Butler / Curator | Done in-room, conversationally. |
| Arrival Unwind + daily card | A short feet-soak / shoulder / breath session; the Curator co-designs the personalised daily plan. | Curator | Plan shared on a printed card and WhatsApp before the guest's first evening. |

**Signature moment:** the arrival ritual and symbolic gift.

### Stage 5 — The Daily Rhythm (Day 1 to penultimate day)

| Time of day | What happens | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Sunrise | Signature tea and fruit; optional group beach yoga or meditation; then the personalised one-on-one (program-specific). | Curator + 2nd teacher |
| Breakfast | Personalised, program-aligned, no printed menu — chef or server confirms preferences. | Chef |
| Mid-morning → mid-day | Free time plus one short daily "education touchpoint" (posture, breath, a sleep micro-routine). | Curator |
| Afternoon | Spa / bodywork with ritual bookends — a foot bath to open, herbal tea to close — paced, never painful back-to-back. | Therapist |
| Sunset | The sunset ritual (beach walk, restorative yoga or yoga nidra); quiet. | Curator |
| Dinner | Personalised, no-menu; herbal tisane and a sleep-support practice; the "tomorrow's plan" message. | Chef + Curator |

**Daily standards:** a whole-team briefing every morning so butler, chef, therapist and teachers all know each guest's plan and dietary needs. Fresh flowers refreshed daily; barefoot / shoes-off where appropriate; full, unhurried session times.

**Signature moments:** the no-menu personalised cuisine (the #1 review-driver) and the daily one-on-one.

### Stage 6 — The Signature Peak (mid-stay)

| Touchpoint | What happens | Owner | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunset sea / lamp (deepam) ceremony | A culturally rooted ceremony at sunset on the beach — a lamp and flower offering to the sea, intention-setting, gentle chant or breath. | Curator (+ local practitioner) | Pure hospitality, no medical claim. The thing they tell friends about. |
| Konkan flower bath | A sensory, photographable herbal and flower bath using local botanicals. | Therapist | The signature spa moment — the most-photographed beat of the stay. |

### Stage 7 — Departure & Integration (final day)

| Touchpoint | What happens | Owner | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Closing session + progress debrief | Review what shifted (sleep, mobility, energy, clarity) and set 2–3 sustainable home practices. | Curator | A genuine debrief, not a checkout formality. |
| Take-home routine | A personalised printed card plus a short video sequence. | Curator | Something they can actually follow at home. |
| Recipe cards + apothecary gift | A few signature recipes plus a Kairali product. | Chef + Concierge | Guests at the benchmarks leave wanting the recipes — give them. |
| Feedback ask + rebooking seed | A gentle feedback request; suggest the next program or progression; a returning-guest gesture. | Curator | Warm, not transactional. |

**Signature moment:** the recipe cards and home routine.

### Stage 8 — Post-Stay & Advocacy (T+1 to T+28)

| Touchpoint | What happens | Owner | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24–48h thank-you + feedback | A personal note and a short feedback form on program outcomes (sleep, pain, mood, clarity). | Curator | Personal, never a generic blast. |
| Review request | Timed after positive feedback, with a direct link. | Marketing | Easy to leave; never falsely incentivised. |
| 2–4 week follow-up check-in | How are the home practices holding; optional video consult. | Curator | Almost no Goa competitor does this — a genuine differentiator. |
| Next-stay / progression / referral | A tailored next-program suggestion and a referral gesture. | Marketing + Curator | Specific to the guest's journey, not a mailshot. |

**Signature moment:** the 2–4 week follow-up — it closes the loop and drives both rebooking and referral.

## 6. Program overlays

All programs run the same eight-stage spine; only the overlay changes (pre-arrival focus, daily one-on-one, cuisine angle, signature ritual, take-home).

**Joint Revival (14–21 nights, min 7):** arthritis / chronic stiffness / post-injury / desk work. Therapeutic yoga + gentle surf/aqua mobility; warm-oil bodywork. Anti-inflammatory cuisine. Konkan flower bath + gentle sea-mobility walk. Take-home: mobility video routine, 30-day check-in.

**Mind Renewal (7–10 nights, min 7):** burnout, high-stress professionals, creatives, life transitions. Breath-led yoga, yoga nidra, meditation, journaling. Cognitive-clarity cuisine; lighter evenings. Sunset sea/lamp ceremony + sound healing. Take-home: 10-minute daily mental-hygiene routine, reading/audio kit.

**Metabolic Reset (10–14 nights, min 7):** low energy, digestive issues, weight-management, post-illness fatigue. Activation yoga, coastal cardio, mindful-nutrition session with the chef. Personalised metabolic menu (no-menu model). Cook-with-the-chef session + sleep-optimisation ritual. Take-home: nutrition principles, busy-day movement protocol.

**Customized (flexible):** grief journeys, couples reconnection, creative sabbaticals, post-surgery recovery. Pre-arrival video consult; stay co-designed to a brief; fully bespoke cuisine and rituals; bespoke integration plan.

**Coastal Reset (2–3 nights, min 2):** short-escape rest. Light intake; group sunrise yoga + one personalised session; coastal-wellness cuisine; one signature ritual; simple reset card.

## 8. Cross-cutting systems & standards

- **The named Curator model.** One person owns each guest's outcome from booking to T+28. The single most important structural change — and Villa Raag's current gap.
- **The WhatsApp concierge thread.** Dedicated per-guest channel for the Curator relationship, logistics and daily plan.
- **The whole-team daily briefing.** Every morning the team aligns on each guest's plan and dietary needs.
- **The cuisine personalisation workflow.** Intake → chef brief (pre-arrival) → chef meets guest on arrival → daily confirmation → recipe cards at departure.
- **The feedback & review loop.** 24–48h feedback → timed review request → 2–4 week follow-up → next-stay/referral.
- **The service-recovery protocol.** When something breaks — especially food or noise — acknowledge fast, fix, and make a gesture; never argue.

**Standards restated (the design-out list):** test acoustic privacy before peak season (the #1 risk at this scale); a guest should never have to ask for everything; pace treatments; zero up-selling pressure; hold minimum-stay discipline; keep therapeutic and short-escape tiers distinct.
