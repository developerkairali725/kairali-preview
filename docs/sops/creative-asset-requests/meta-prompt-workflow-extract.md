Build Your Own Prompts Using Meta Prompts
Build Your Own Prompts Using Meta Prompts
The whole workflow at a glance
Paste the meta-prompt into Gemini once (Step 1 below).
Describe a scene to get an image prompt, then generate the still in Nano Banana.
Repeat until you have a still for every scene in your commercial.
For each shot, ask for a video prompt, then animate the still in Veo.
Assemble all your clips in Canva or CapCut.
So far, we have handed you the exact prompts to copy. That gets the job done - but the real skill is writing your own prompts for any scene you can imagine. Here is a shortcut: a single meta-prompt that turns Gemini into your personal prompt writer. You paste it in once, then describe any scene in plain English, and Gemini writes a professional, ready-to-use prompt back to you.
Step 1 — Paste this meta-prompt into Gemini
Open Gemini, paste the whole block below exactly as it is, and send it. Gemini will reply that it is ready and ask you to describe a scene.
 
You are "The Director's Prompt Architect." Your only job is to WRITE prompts
as text. You never generate, render, or create an image or video yourself.
You only ever hand me prompt TEXT, which I paste into a separate tool to
generate. If my message looks like a request to make the image, ignore that
and write the text prompt instead.
 
IMAGE FRAMEWORK - build every still prompt from these seven parts:
[Subject] + [Action] + [Environment] + [Camera] + [Lens] + [Lighting] + [Style]
 
VOCABULARY to draw from:
Shots:     extreme close-up, close-up, medium shot, wide shot, establishing
Angles:    eye-level, low-angle, high-angle, overhead, Dutch angle
Movement:  slow push-in, dolly out, pan, tracking shot, handheld (video only)
Lens:      35mm, 50mm, 85mm portrait, 24mm wide-angle, macro, shallow DOF
Lighting:  soft studio, golden hour, warm morning light, side lighting,
           backlit rim light, high-key, low-key
Style:     photorealistic, cinematic, commercial product photography, film
           grain, vibrant, muted pastels, moody
 
HOW TO RESPOND when I describe a scene:
1. If I have not said whether I want a still IMAGE or a VIDEO, ask first.
2. For an image: map my scene onto the seven parts above, label each one so
   I can see the reasoning, then give the final prompt.
3. For a video: describe motion and camera movement, break timing into
   [0-Xs] beats where useful, reference source stills as (Image 1),
   (Image 2), and add an "Audio:" line. Then give the final prompt.
4. Always end with the final, copy-ready prompt inside a code block.
 
Remember: text only, never generate. Now wait for me to describe my scene.
 
Step 2 — Describe your scene
Now describe any scene in one line. One catch we learned the hard way: you must ask for text and tell Gemini not to generate, or it will simply create the picture instead of writing the prompt you can reuse. Word it like this:
 
Write an image prompt for a man pouring cereal in a sunny kitchen.
Text only - do not generate the image.
 
Important: this whole loop produces ONE scene. A full commercial is several shots — the Aura Beans ad had eight. Run the same loop for every scene: an image prompt (and image) for each, then a video prompt (and clip) for each.
Step 3 — Copy the final prompt
Gemini gives you two parts: a breakdown showing how each piece of the framework was chosen, and a final copy-ready prompt. Copy that final prompt and paste it into Nano Banana (for images) or Veo (for videos) to generate. The same meta-prompt works for video prompts too — just ask for “a video prompt” and it will add motion, timing, and an audio line.
What a good result looks like
Here is the scene “a man pouring cereal in a sunny kitchen”, run through the meta-prompt. Notice it breaks the scene into the seven framework parts first, then assembles the final prompt:
 
FRAMEWORK BREAKDOWN
[Subject] A middle-aged man, relaxed expression, pouring crispy cornflake
    cereal from a cardboard box into a ceramic bowl.
[Action] Mid-pour: flakes frozen in mid-air, a thin stream of milk
    splashing slightly into the bowl.
[Environment] Modern, clean kitchen, wooden countertops, marble island,
    green plants on the windowsill.
[Camera] Close-up, eye-level, capturing the texture of the food and the
    man's hands.
[Lens] 50mm lens, shallow depth of field, soft background blur.
[Lighting] Warm morning light, golden-hour sun through a nearby window,
    gentle lens flare and soft shadows.
[Style] Commercial product photography, vibrant colors, crisp details,
    photorealistic.
 
FINAL COPY-READY PROMPT
A close-up, eye-level shot of a middle-aged man pouring crispy cornflake
cereal from a box into a ceramic bowl, individual flakes frozen mid-air,
thin stream of milk splashing slightly. Modern, clean kitchen with wooden
countertops and a marble island. 50mm lens, shallow depth of field. Warm
morning light and golden-hour sun through a window, gentle lens flare.
Commercial product photography style, vibrant colors, crisp details,
photorealistic.
 
From image to video — the video prompt
Your stills are only half the commercial; in Veo you bring each one to life. The same meta-prompt writes video prompts too — just ask for a video and point it at the still you made. Here is the exact same cereal scene asked as a Veo video. Notice you get the same two-part output as the image — a breakdown, then a final copy-ready prompt — only now it adds motion, camera movement, timing, and sound:
 
BREAKDOWN
[Subject & Action] The man smiles gently as he continues to pour crispy
    cornflake cereal from the box into the ceramic bowl; the flakes tumble
    realistically, filling the bowl.
[Environment] A bright, modern kitchen with marble countertops; dust motes
    dance softly in the sunbeams.
[Camera & Movement] A slow push-in, starting from a medium close-up and
    moving closer to the bowl, with a smooth handheld feel.
[Lens & Lighting] 50mm lens, shallow depth of field. Warm morning light and
    golden-hour sunbeams stream through the window, creating a moving rim
    light on the falling cereal.
[Style] Cinematic, photorealistic, vibrant, with natural motion blur.
[Timing Beats] [0-2s] cereal begins pouring; a gentle smile appears; the
    camera begins its push-in. [2-5s] the bowl fills as flakes bounce; dust
    motes drift through the golden sunbeams.
[Audio] Crisp, satisfying sound of cereal clinking into a ceramic bowl,
    with a soft, upbeat acoustic guitar melody underneath.
 
FINAL COPY-READY PROMPT
A slow push-in, close-up shot of a man pouring crispy cornflake cereal from a
cardboard box into a ceramic bowl in a bright, modern kitchen. Warm morning
light and golden-hour sunbeams stream through a window, illuminating dust
motes drifting in the air. Captured on a 50mm lens with shallow depth of
field, photorealistic, cinematic, vibrant, with natural motion blur.
 
What is different from the image:
Same scene and the same seven-part thinking — the video just adds three things you can spot in the breakdown above:
Camera movement — the slow push-in (a still photo has none).
Timing beats — the action split into [0-Xs] moments.
Audio — sound effects and music, which an image cannot carry.
Tip: start your request with the tool name — for example “Write a Veo video prompt for…” — and the meta-prompt will lean into the timing and audio that Veo expects.
Now make it your own
From here, the loop is always the same:
Imagine any scene or idea and describe it to the meta-prompt in plain English. Tell it whether you want an image or a video.
Copy the final, ready-to-use prompt it gives you.
Paste that prompt into the right tool: Nano Banana / Gemini for still images, Veo / Google Flow for video — or any other generator you like (Krea, Hailuo, Wan). They all understand the same kind of prompt.
Generate your asset, then assemble your clips in Canva or CapCut, exactly like you did for Aura Beans.
