## 4. Systemic risks + strengths

### Risks (GPT's synthesis, calibrated against verification)

GPT's step-7 `overall` object listed 12 systemic risks (verbatim in the transcript). Calibrated:

1. **Upheld — the strongest theme: "policy-heavy, physics-light" at the edges.** The OS's *core* spine is mechanized (front gates, EV-4, frozen lockfile, lighthouse-gate, authenticated approvals, fail-closed change scoping — all verified), but a ring of obligations still binds conduct only: session-end rules (GPTW-03), issue state machine (GPTW-25), DIRECTIVES immutability (GPTW-26), single-writer (GPTW-04/36/42), EV-2 fencing (GPTW-34), model routing (GPTW-19). Each is individually small; together they are the OS's largest honest attack surface, and most share one property: *the lint that closes them is cheap*.
2. **Upheld — the guard-scope asymmetry (GPTW-15).** Bash-line pattern-matching cannot see inside a Node process. This is the one risk in the register that needs *design*, not a lint.
3. **Upheld — the telemetry hole (GPTW-44/45).** Cost budgets are declared first-class but nothing measures actuals; "budget-gated per job, fleet-blind" is an accurate summary.
4. **Partially upheld — external-review availability (GPTW-46).** Fail-closed ship gates hold; the residual truth is that *review-type tasks* (this leg itself) wait on API/owner availability with only handover-queue pressure.
5. **Discounted after verification:** four of the twelve risk lines dissolve on contact with the mechanisms — evidence immutability and manifest integrity (EV-4 is merge-blocking), Lane-B resolver wiring (in CI), HG1/HG2 CI exposure (no production deploy lane exists), review-evidence provenance (hash-bound). These were reasonable inferences from a dossier that under-described the CI wiring; they are recorded here as adjudicated, not silently dropped.
