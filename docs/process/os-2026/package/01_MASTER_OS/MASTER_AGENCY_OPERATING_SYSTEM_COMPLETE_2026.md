# Digital Growth Agency Operating System - 2026

**Version:** 2.0  
**Issued:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope:** new builds, upgrades, redesigns, replatforming, migrations, organic growth, recovery, campaign/evergreen landing pages, B2C, B2B, D2C, Shopify, local and international operations.

## 1. Purpose

This operating system replaces a collection of disconnected agency audits with one governed lifecycle. It is designed so a competent human team or tool-enabled AI agent can discover the current state, protect existing equity, diagnose constraints, design a solution, implement it, prove implementation, launch safely, monitor outcomes and improve continuously.

It is not a promise that a finite document contains literally every book, paper, video or forum post. That claim would be impossible to verify and would make the system less trustworthy. Instead, the OS contains:

- a comprehensive control system covering the material workstreams used by high-end digital agencies;
- a curated evidence library with source IDs, dates and confidence classes;
- an explicit excluded/deferred/not-applicable register so omitted ideas have reasons;
- an update process for incorporating future standards, research and platform changes;
- machine-readable schemas, evidence manifests and acceptance gates.

## 2. Governing outcomes

Every project must protect or improve all applicable outcomes:

1. qualified market visibility across conventional search, generative search, local, shopping, marketplaces, paid media and referrals;
2. revenue, contribution margin, qualified pipeline and customer lifetime value;
3. URL, content, ranking, link, review, tracking and audience equity;
4. user task success, accessibility, trust and brand positioning;
5. technical reliability, security, privacy and recoverability;
6. regional, language, currency, inventory, fulfilment and legal correctness;
7. measurement integrity and proof that work was actually completed.

## 3. Non-negotiable operating rules

- [ ] Never begin with design. Begin with business economics, market definition, evidence access and the protected baseline.
- [ ] Never remove, merge or rename a URL until its traffic, queries, conversions, revenue, links, internal links, ads, emails, offline references and historical purpose have been checked.
- [ ] Never replace a proven landing page without keeping the original as a control or preserving a recoverable copy and baseline.
- [ ] Never optimize a third-party score when first-party revenue or user evidence contradicts it.
- [ ] Never call work complete because code was written or a ticket was marked done. Completion requires deployment, live retest and stored evidence.
- [ ] Never use competitor copying as strategy. Identify the customer problem, economic opportunity and defensible advantage.
- [ ] Never use Reddit, YouTube or a vendor benchmark as the only evidence for a high-impact decision.
- [ ] Never let an AI agent autonomously publish, migrate, redirect, delete, disavow, change tracking, alter checkout or modify legal/medical claims without the required approval gate.
- [ ] Never overwrite raw baseline files. Preserve immutable dated copies and hashes.
- [ ] Never launch with unresolved P0 issues. P1 exceptions require named accountable acceptance and expiry date.

## 5. Phase-gated lifecycle

| Phase | Question answered | Mandatory output | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Classify and charter | What work is being requested, for which market/domain and who is accountable? | Project charter, workflow selection, market/domain matrix, access/risk register | Ambiguous scope, no accountable owner or no legal authority |
| 1. Baseline and discovery | What exists, what works, what is at risk and what is unknown? | Commercial, URL, sitemap, content, ranking, backlink, UX, tracking, technical and competitor baselines | Missing commercial truth, incomplete URL discovery or unrecorded data gaps |
| 2. Diagnose and prioritize | What is constraining growth and how confident are we? | Issue register, causal hypotheses, opportunity model, competitor gap and preservation register | Recommendations without evidence, impact or acceptance criteria |
| 3. Strategy and specification | What exact future state should be built? | Sitemap, URL policy, page briefs, content/UX/CRO/SEO/analytics/technical specifications | Design without page purpose, measurement or protected elements |
| 4. Build and controlled implementation | Was the approved specification implemented on a controlled environment? | Versioned build, content, integrations, tracking, release notes, issue links | Untraceable changes or production edits without rollback |
| 5. Pre-launch QA and rehearsal | Does it work, preserve equity and meet release gates? | Crawls, automated tests, manual task tests, migration rehearsal, launch certificate | Open P0; unaccepted P1; failed conversion, tracking, redirect, security or rollback test |
| 6. Launch command centre | Is the live release correct in every target market? | Deployment log, real conversion, live crawl, sitemap/feed/ad verification, incident log | Guardrail breach or inability to roll back |
| 7. Stabilize and verify | Did rankings, indexation, revenue, conversion and operations remain within guardrails? | 24h/72h/7d/28d comparison, root-cause fixes and acceptance | Unexplained material regression |

## 6. Evidence hierarchy

1. **Commercial truth:** finance, backend orders, contribution margin, CRM stages, qualified calls, refunds, returns and repeat purchase.
2. **First-party platform evidence:** Search Console, GA4, GTM, Google Ads, Merchant Center, Business Profile, server logs, Shopify Analytics, form/payment/email logs.
3. **Controlled tests:** usability studies, experiments, QA, browser/device tests, Playwright, accessibility and security tests.
4. **Diagnostic platforms:** Screaming Frog, Semrush, DataForSEO, Ahrefs, Similarweb, PageSpeed, CrUX, GTmetrix, WebPageTest, Clarity/Hotjar.
5. **External benchmarks and estimates:** industry medians, traffic estimates, search volume, keyword difficulty, DA/DR/Authority Score, AI visibility estimates.
6. **Anecdotal discovery evidence:** Reddit, forums, YouTube, practitioner case reports. These generate questions and failure-mode checks; they do not prove a universal rule.

## 7. Benchmark and target algorithm

For every KPI, identify the applicable benchmark class:

- **HARD:** standards or zero-defect controls, such as valid HTTPS, no P0 broken conversion path, WCAG conformance target, or Core Web Vitals thresholds.
- **OWN:** the organisation’s trailing 12-16 month seasonal baseline, cohort and device/region distribution.
- **PEER:** named competitor median, upper quartile and leader by the same market, device and intent.
- **INDUSTRY:** a large-sample published benchmark with year, sample and definition.
- **ECONOMIC:** the minimum performance required by margin, CAC, sales capacity or payback.
- **EXPERIMENTAL:** a hypothesis-specific target defined by minimum detectable effect and decision rule.

A target is valid only when the metric definition, source, segment, baseline period, benchmark date, confidence and owner are recorded. Generic “industry average” targets are rejected when the conversion definition, market or traffic mix differs materially.

## 8. Competitive growth doctrine

Competitive intelligence is embedded in every phase. The system distinguishes:

- direct commercial competitors;
- organic SERP competitors;
- paid-search and paid-social competitors;
- marketplace and retailer competitors;
- local/map competitors;
- substitute solutions and category alternatives;
- content/publisher attention competitors;
- AI-answer recommendation competitors;
- reputation and review competitors;
- aspirational design and experience benchmarks.

The objective is not to imitate all of them. The decision must be one of: **defend**, **match a mandatory parity factor**, **flank an underserved segment**, **attack a vulnerable demand pool**, **create a category**, **partner**, or **deliberately ignore**.

## 9. Opportunity economics

Use a documented model:

`Expected incremental contribution = addressable demand x attainable share x qualified conversion rate x contribution value x confidence - incremental cost`

Prioritize using:

`Priority score = incremental contribution x strategic importance x confidence x reach / (effort x time-to-value x risk)`

Every input must identify whether it is observed, calculated, estimated or assumed. A sensitivity range is mandatory for high-value bets.

## 10. Definition of done

An issue or deliverable is complete only when all are true:

1. requirement and acceptance criteria exist;
2. implementation is linked to an issue/change ID;
3. code/content/configuration is committed or versioned;
4. automated and manual checks pass;
5. the change is deployed to the intended environment;
6. the live output is retested;
7. evidence is archived with tool/version/configuration, timestamp and hash;
8. the relevant KPI is monitored for the specified period;
9. no unexplained regression exists;
10. the reviewer approves, or a named owner accepts residual risk.

# Competitive Intelligence and Market-Share Capture Playbook - 2026

## 1. Purpose

This playbook turns competitor research into an evidence-backed growth programme. “Eat into market share” means winning a defined group of qualified customers, demand, transactions or attention profitably. It does not mean copying competitors, attacking their infrastructure, creating false reviews, manipulating links or making unsupported comparative claims.

## 2. Competitive intelligence lifecycle

1. Define the exact market and denominator.
2. Build a multi-channel competitor universe.
3. Capture evidence using the same period, market, language and device.
4. Separate facts, estimates, calculations and assumptions.
5. Diagnose parity gaps, competitor weaknesses and defendable advantages.
6. Quantify the opportunity and cost.
7. Select defend, match, flank, attack, create, partner or ignore.
8. Build the page/product/channel changes and acceptance criteria.
9. Test or launch with commercial guardrails.
10. Monitor competitor reactions, market share and profitability.

## 4. Competitor roster taxonomy

For every target market, maintain separate lists for:

- commercial competitors selling the same outcome;
- substitutes solving the problem differently;
- organic search competitors ranking for priority queries;
- paid competitors bidding or advertising into the audience;
- marketplaces, retailers, aggregators and distributors;
- publishers, forums, YouTube channels and creators controlling attention;
- businesses recommended by AI systems;
- local/map competitors by physical location or service area;
- aspirational experience/brand references that are not direct commercial rivals.

A domain can be a strong SEO competitor and a weak commercial competitor, or vice versa. The roster must therefore record the competitor type and market rather than using one undifferentiated list.

## 5. Market-share attack strategy

### 5.1 Defend
Protect branded demand, best-converting pages, high-margin products, link-earning assets, locations, reviews and customer cohorts. Fix migration and tracking risk before attacking.

### 5.2 Match mandatory parity
Close blockers customers now expect: mobile usability, credible proof, clear price/terms, delivery, returns, availability, page speed, search/filter, accessibility and customer support. Parity is not differentiation; it prevents avoidable loss.

### 5.3 Flank
Target a profitable segment competitors underserve: location, language, audience, use case, price/value tier, product format, urgency, channel, information need or service model.

### 5.4 Attack a vulnerable demand pool
Build a coordinated offer, page, content, link/PR, paid, local, merchant and lifecycle plan around a quantified cluster. Define expected incremental contribution, capacity and guardrails.

### 5.5 Create a category or moat
Use proprietary research, clinical/technical expertise, first-party data, unique products, tools, service processes, communities, partnerships and credible proof that competitors cannot easily copy.

### 5.6 Ignore deliberately
Record opportunities that are low-margin, legally risky, operationally impossible, outside brand strategy or based on weak estimates. Include a reconsideration trigger.

## 6. Opportunity scorecard

For each opportunity capture:

- market/region/language/location;
- customer segment and job-to-be-done;
- demand size and source;
- current own share and competitor shares;
- attainable share range;
- conversion and contribution assumptions;
- capacity constraint;
- time to value;
- confidence class;
- legal/brand/platform risk;
- required pages, products, content, links, ads, localization and operations;
- expected incremental contribution under downside/base/upside cases;
- owner and next evidence required.

## 7. Competitor monitoring cadence

- **Daily/weekly:** paid creative, pricing/availability, major rankings, local reviews, outages, launches and high-priority changes.
- **Monthly:** SOV, traffic/channel estimates, backlinks, content, offer, SERP/AI visibility and landing-page changes.
- **Quarterly:** complete market map, peer percentiles, strategic position, customer research and attack backlog.
- **Event-driven:** funding, acquisition, regulation, new market entry, product recall, domain migration, brand crisis or major algorithm/platform change.

## 8. Ethical and legal red lines

Do not conduct unauthorized access, personal-data collection, evasion of technical restrictions, negative SEO, fake reviews, impersonation, trademark misuse, content theft, price coordination, fabricated comparisons or undisclosed sponsored advocacy. Use public, licensed, client-owned or properly consented evidence. Legal review is mandatory for comparative claims, regulated categories and sensitive data.

# Metrics, Tools, Benchmarks and Solution Dictionary - 2026

## 1. How to use this dictionary

Every report and issue must answer seven questions:

1. **What is the metric and exact formula?**
2. **What dimensions are mandatory?** Region, domain, language, page/template, device, source, cohort and period are not optional when they materially change interpretation.
3. **What is the source of truth?** A third-party estimate cannot overrule finance, CRM, Search Console or server evidence.
4. **What benchmark class applies?** Hard standard, own baseline, peer percentile, industry study, economic requirement or experiment.
5. **How far behind or ahead are we?** Show absolute and percentage gap, confidence and comparability.
6. **What diagnosis should be performed before recommending a change?** Metrics do not identify causes by themselves.
7. **What exact improvement, acceptance test and monitoring period are required?**

## 2. Target-setting protocol

- Use a hard threshold only where an authoritative standard defines one.
- Use the client’s seasonal and cohort baseline as the default comparator for business performance.
- Build peer percentiles from the named relevant competitor set using identical definitions.
- Use industry benchmarks only as context and record sample, year, sector and conversion definition.
- Derive commercial targets from contribution economics, capacity and payback.
- Use experiments for uncertain causal changes; define minimum detectable effect and guardrails before launch.
- Record `not comparable` rather than forcing a misleading benchmark.

## 6. Gap calculation

For higher-is-better metrics:

`Gap to target = target - current`  
`Relative gap = (target - current) / target`

For lower-is-better metrics:

`Gap to target = current - target`

For competitor percentiles, store the competitor set, values, median, upper quartile, leader, collection date, market and method. Do not compare values created by different tools without normalization.

## 7. Diagnostic rule

A metric crossing a threshold creates an investigation, not an automatic solution. The issue record must connect:

`observation -> affected segment -> corroborating evidence -> likely mechanism -> proposed intervention -> acceptance test -> commercial guardrail`

The complete row-level definitions, required dimensions, red flags, diagnosis, recommended actions and prohibited shortcuts are in `07_TEMPLATES/METRIC_DICTIONARY.csv`.

# Evidence, Storage, QA and AI-Agent Execution Standard - 2026

## 1. Objective

The operating system must prove not only what was recommended, but what was observed, changed, deployed, verified and learned. A PDF is useful for human review but is not sufficient alone. Preserve the raw machine-readable data, configuration and provenance needed to reproduce the finding.

## 2. Three evidence layers

1. **RAW:** immutable exports exactly as collected - CSV, JSON, crawl files, HAR, screenshots, videos, PDFs, logs and platform exports.
2. **PROCESSED:** normalized tables, joins, calculated metrics and notebooks/scripts. Every processed artifact references its raw inputs.
3. **PUBLISHED:** client reports, dashboards, issue summaries and decisions. These reference processed calculations and raw evidence IDs.

Never edit or overwrite RAW evidence. Corrections create a new run ID.

## 3. Required storage path

```text
clients/{client_id}/projects/{project_id}/
  00_governance/
  01_raw/{YYYY}/{MM}/{DD}/{source}/{report_type}/{run_id}/
  02_processed/{dataset}/{version}/
  03_findings/{issue_id}/
  04_specifications/
  05_implementation/{release_id}/
  06_qa/{release_id}/{environment}/
  07_launch/{release_id}/
  08_monitoring/{cadence}/{period}/
  09_published_reports/{version}/
  10_archive/
```

The folder may live in approved object storage, Google Drive and/or a Git repository depending on artifact type. Large binary raw reports belong in durable object/document storage; code, schemas, manifests and small text evidence belong in version control. Sensitive data must follow access, retention and deletion policies.

## 4. Artifact manifest

Every report run or evidence bundle must include:

- artifact ID and run ID;
- client/project/release/issue identifiers;
- source/tool and version;
- exact configuration, API endpoint/query or crawl settings;
- domain/property, environment and URL scope;
- market, country, location, language, currency, device and user agent;
- date range, comparison range, timezone and collection timestamp;
- operator or agent identity and model/version when applicable;
- raw file names, MIME types, sizes and SHA-256 hashes;
- known exclusions, sampling, failures and data gaps;
- upstream input IDs and downstream issue/report IDs;
- review and approval status.

This follows the W3C PROV idea of recording entities, activities and agents so trust and reproducibility can be assessed [W03].

## 5. Historical report policy

- Save each human-readable report as PDF when the source supports it.
- Save the corresponding CSV/JSON/API response and settings; PDF alone is insufficient.
- Never overwrite a monthly or pre-launch report. Use a new timestamp/version.
- Generate a checksum manifest after collection.
- Store baseline screenshots and critical-page full-page captures.
- For dynamic dashboards, export the underlying data and save the dashboard definition or query.
- Record tool plan/licence limitations and unavailable dimensions.
- Retain pre-migration evidence and redirect maps for the approved retention period, normally at least the useful life of the migration and any legal/contractual requirement.

## 6. Issue evidence bundle

Every issue folder contains:

1. issue record and severity;
2. raw evidence or pointers to artifact IDs;
3. affected URL/entity export;
4. business consequence and confidence;
5. recommendation and acceptance criteria;
6. implementation link: commit, PR, CMS revision, tag version, configuration or vendor ticket;
7. staging test evidence;
8. live deployment evidence;
9. post-deployment retest;
10. KPI monitoring result;
11. final reviewer decision or accepted risk.

## 7. Proof-of-completion state machine

`OPEN -> EVIDENCED -> APPROVED -> IMPLEMENTING -> STAGING_QA -> READY_FOR_RELEASE -> DEPLOYED -> LIVE_VERIFIED -> MONITORED -> CLOSED`

A ticket may not skip directly from IMPLEMENTING to CLOSED. A failed live test returns it to IMPLEMENTING or triggers rollback. P0/P1 closure requires independent review.

## 8. Automated release checks

Where technically possible, require:

- build/unit/integration tests;
- Playwright critical journeys and traces;
- visual snapshot comparison under controlled environment [PW01];
- redirect-map tests and no-loop checks;
- internal broken-link scan;
- robots/noindex/canonical/hreflang/schema tests;
- analytics event and ecommerce payload tests;
- axe-core accessibility scan plus manual critical-journey checks;
- Lighthouse CI/performance budgets;
- security/dependency scans;
- sitemap validation;
- market/language/currency/inventory tests;
- backup and rollback verification.

Protect the release branch and require reviews/checks. Build provenance or artifact attestations may be used to verify where/how release artifacts were produced [GH01-GH02].

## 9. AI-agent operating contract

An AI agent must receive:

- workflow ID and phase;
- client/project/release IDs;
- exact domain, market, language, device and date scope;
- authorized tools and credentials boundaries;
- input artifact IDs and freshness requirements;
- required output schema;
- evidence and citation requirements;
- prohibited actions;
- approval gate and escalation owner;
- retry/timeout/cost limits;
- definition of done.

### Agent evidence labels

Every statement must be labelled internally as one of:

- `OBSERVED_FIRST_PARTY`
- `OBSERVED_LIVE_TEST`
- `CALCULATED`
- `THIRD_PARTY_ESTIMATE`
- `USER_PROVIDED`
- `EXTERNAL_BENCHMARK`
- `ANECDOTAL_SIGNAL`
- `ASSUMPTION`
- `RECOMMENDATION`
- `UNKNOWN`

### Prohibited autonomous actions

Without the specified human gate, an agent must not:

- publish or delete production content;
- change domains, DNS, robots, canonicals, hreflang or redirects;
- submit disavow files;
- change analytics definitions, consent or payment logic;
- install/uninstall Shopify apps or alter checkout;
- make regulated medical, legal or financial claims;
- send competitor outreach, reviews or public communications;
- expose secrets or customer data;
- approve its own P0/P1 evidence.

Agentic systems should also be threat-modelled for prompt injection, tool misuse, insecure memory/context and excessive autonomy, using current agent-security guidance [O02].

## 10. Exclusion and decision register

Every suggested control, finding or opportunity receives one status:

- **INCLUDED_NOW** - applicable and scheduled;
- **DEFERRED** - applicable but delayed, with risk and reconsideration date;
- **NOT_APPLICABLE** - reason tied to business model, technology, market or absence of feature;
- **REJECTED** - evidence or economics do not support it;
- **UNKNOWN** - evidence/access missing; owner and next step required;
- **ACCEPTED_RISK** - accountable owner accepts consequence until expiry.

Record source, rationale, risk, owner, approval, expiry and reconsideration trigger. This prevents future reviewers or AIs from repeatedly proposing an idea that was already evaluated, while allowing decisions to be reopened when assumptions change.

## 11. What must not count as proof

- a task status without live retest;
- a screenshot without URL, timestamp, environment and expected result;
- an AI-generated summary without raw exports;
- a tool health score without issue-level evidence;
- a PDF without machine-readable data/configuration;
- code committed but not deployed;
- deployment completed but conversion/indexation not verified;
- improved rankings without qualified conversion/revenue analysis;
- a benchmark without source, date, segment and definition;
- “not applicable” without rationale.

# International, Localization, Domain and Location Operating Manual - 2026

## 1. Market matrix before architecture

Create one row per country/region x language x currency x legal entity x fulfilment model x location. Record:

- addressable customers and commercial priority;
- products/services available and excluded;
- price, tax, duty, shipping, returns and payment methods;
- local legal/claims/privacy requirements;
- support hours, phone/WhatsApp/email and response SLA;
- local inventory, booking or practitioner/location capacity;
- domain/subdomain/subfolder and canonical policy;
- language-locale code and hreflang relationship;
- acquisition sources and exact landing destinations;
- Business Profiles, local listings and review platforms;
- translation owner, glossary, style guide and reviewer;
- fallback experience when an offering is unavailable.

## 2. Domain architecture decision

Assess country-code domains, generic domains with subfolders, subdomains and separate brands using SEO equity, governance, technical platform, legal entity, customer trust, local operations, paid tracking, Merchant/Shopify Markets and long-term cost. The decision record must explain rejected architectures.

## 3. Localization workflow

1. Write source content that is factually approved and localization-ready.
2. Create terminology/glossary, prohibited claims, names, units, dates, addresses and tone rules.
3. Translate with context, not strings alone.
4. Localize keyword demand and search intent; do not directly translate keyword lists.
5. Adapt examples, proof, imagery, offers, payment, shipping, support and legal text.
6. Run linguistic, functional, SEO, accessibility and visual QA.
7. Validate URLs, canonicals, hreflang, language selectors and analytics dimensions.
8. Obtain market owner and legal/clinical approval where applicable.
9. Monitor wrong-market landings, switchbacks, local conversion and support issues.

## 4. Technical requirements

- Stable, crawlable locale-specific URLs.
- Self-canonical pages unless a deliberate cross-locale canonical is justified.
- Correct reciprocal hreflang or sitemap annotations, including language-country codes and optional x-default.
- Equivalent page relationships; do not map unrelated pages as alternates.
- Language/currency selector with persistent user choice.
- Avoid opaque forced IP redirection; offer a clear recommendation where useful.
- Local XML sitemaps and Search Console properties as needed.
- Localized metadata, structured data, product/feed, availability and organization/location details.
- Redirect and fallback rules for removed market pages.

## 5. Local location workflow

For each real location/service area capture Business Profile, NAP, categories, hours, booking, photos, reviews, services, local page, directions, landmarks, staff/proof, schema, calls and local rank grid. Do not create doorway pages or fake locations. Location pages must contain genuinely useful local information and an operational conversion path.

## 6. Metrics

Track local organic/paid/AI SOV, local pack grid share, GBP calls/directions/bookings, local-page conversion, wrong-market session rate, locale switching, hreflang validity, translation defects, unavailable product/service landings, local review health, shipping/payment failures and customer-support contacts by language.

## 7. Commonly missed edge controls

- deep links from ads/email/social bypassing market selectors;
- currencies that display correctly but charge differently;
- translated UI with untranslated validation/error/email messages;
- structured data and Merchant feed inconsistent with visible page;
- legal pages or medical claims copied across jurisdictions;
- local phone numbers without routing/recording consent controls;
- hreflang to redirects, noindex or noncanonical URLs;
- locale selector built with non-crawlable JavaScript;
- sold-out/unavailable products still advertised into a market;
- reviews/testimonials that are not applicable or lawful in a market.

# Monitoring, Regression Control and Continuous Improvement Manual - 2026

## 1. Monitoring architecture

Monitoring is arranged in five layers:

1. **Availability and revenue protection:** uptime, payment/form/booking, inventory/feed, DNS/SSL, errors and alerts.
2. **Search eligibility and migration protection:** robots, noindex, canonicals, redirects, sitemap, indexation, crawl and priority rankings.
3. **Experience and conversion:** CWV, JavaScript errors, task funnels, search, form/cart/checkout, accessibility and support incidents.
4. **Commercial quality:** qualified leads, wins, contribution, returns, repeat rate, CAC/payback and cohort LTV.
5. **Competitive position:** SOV, market/channel share estimates, pricing/offers, reviews, links, content, paid creative, local and AI visibility.

## 2. Cadence

| Cadence | Controls | Audience | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real-time/hourly | Uptime, critical synthetic journeys, payment/form failures, 5xx, feed disapprovals, severe security and deployment alerts | Operations/development | Incident or no-notification rule |
| Daily during launch | Revenue/conversions, tracking, redirects, 404/5xx, robots/canonical/sitemap, ads destinations, Merchant Center, critical markets | Launch command centre | Daily launch log and issue actions |
| Weekly for 8-12 weeks | GSC pages/queries, indexation, rank clusters, redirects, backlinks, CWV, funnel, lead/order quality, regional anomalies | Project team | Stabilization scorecard |
| Monthly | Executive commercial KPIs, recrawl, content decay, landing pages, local/merchant, AI/competitive SOV, tests and backlog | Leadership and channel owners | Monthly operating review |
| Quarterly | Full competitor map, technical/content/UX/accessibility/security audit, architecture, localization and tool health | Steering group | Quarterly growth plan |
| Annual/pre-rebuild | Complete baseline, market strategy, platform architecture, data governance and risk review | Executive sponsor | Approved annual roadmap or rebuild charter |

## 3. Alert design

Every alert records metric, scope, baseline method, threshold, minimum duration, owner, notification channel, diagnostic runbook and rollback authority. Use absolute and relative thresholds where appropriate. Suppress known seasonality and planned campaign effects only through documented maintenance windows.

### Default P0 alert examples

- production unavailable or critical conversion fails twice from independent checks;
- payment/form/booking success drops to zero or backend delivery fails;
- production homepage or major template becomes `noindex`, blocked or canonically points elsewhere;
- widespread 5xx or DNS/SSL failure;
- material revenue or qualified-lead collapse after release beyond approved guardrail;
- critical security finding or data exposure;
- wrong-market pricing/claims/availability with material customer impact.

### P1 examples

- priority pages lose indexation or redirects fail;
- significant GSC click/impression decline not explained by demand/seasonality;
- Core Web Vitals priority template becomes poor;
- Merchant/product disapprovals spike;
- checkout/form abandonment or error rate rises materially;
- review sentiment or local visibility deteriorates in priority location;
- competitor captures a strategically important demand cluster.

## 4. Regression comparison

Compare:

- last run versus previous run;
- post-release versus pre-release baseline;
- current period versus seasonally comparable period;
- actual versus target/guardrail;
- own site versus named peers using identical methodology;
- mobile versus desktop;
- each market/language/domain/location;
- each page template and traffic source;
- new versus returning customer and relevant cohorts.

A regression report must list added, removed and changed URLs; status/canonical/directive/title/H1/schema/internal-link differences; performance changes; conversion changes; and unexplained gaps.

## 5. Continuous improvement loop

1. Observe a metric, customer problem, competitor move or operational failure.
2. Validate the data and segment.
3. Diagnose the mechanism using multiple evidence sources.
4. Quantify commercial impact and confidence.
5. Prioritize with the opportunity score.
6. Define hypothesis, change, primary metric, guardrails and acceptance criteria.
7. Implement a controlled change.
8. QA tracking and user experience before interpreting results.
9. Adopt, iterate, revert or reject.
10. Store the learning, update the decision/exclusion register and refine the standard.

## 6. Experiment governance

Experiments require stable assignment, correct exposure logging, sample-size/power method, pre-defined primary metric, guardrails, stopping rule and data-quality check. Do not stop because the graph looks favorable, repeatedly inspect and cherry-pick, or ignore novelty/seasonality. When randomization is impossible, use a clearly labelled quasi-experimental or time-series design and state limitations.

## 7. Executive scorecard

The top page should normally contain no more than 10-15 decision metrics:

- revenue/contribution or qualified pipeline;
- revenue per session or value per qualified visit;
- conversion and lead/order quality;
- CAC/payback/LTV or equivalent economics;
- organic/paid/AI/local visibility in priority demand;
- priority indexation/technical health;
- Core Web Vitals pass rate for valuable templates;
- customer/review/return/service health;
- market-share proxy trend;
- open P0/P1 and accepted risks;
- progress of strategic attack initiatives.

Detailed metrics remain in appendices and dashboards.
