# Master Operating System

**Version:** 1.0  
**Issued:** 2026-07-19  
**Scope:** This standard is the governing core for new builds, upgrades, redesigns, replatforming, migrations, organic growth, recovery, campaign and evergreen landing pages, B2C, B2B, D2C, Shopify, local and international operations. It establishes the phase gates, approval authority, evidence rules, benchmark classes, decision states and definition of done inherited by every workflow.  
**Referenced by:** `B2C-NEW-WEBSITE`, `B2C-EXISTING-WEBSITE`, `B2C-NEW-ORGANIC`, `B2C-EXISTING-ORGANIC`, `B2C-NEW-LANDING`, `B2C-EXISTING-LANDING`; `B2B-NEW-WEBSITE`, `B2B-EXISTING-WEBSITE`, `B2B-NEW-ORGANIC`, `B2B-EXISTING-ORGANIC`, `B2B-NEW-LANDING`, `B2B-EXISTING-LANDING`; `D2C-NEW-WEBSITE`, `D2C-EXISTING-WEBSITE`, `D2C-NEW-ORGANIC`, `D2C-EXISTING-ORGANIC`, `D2C-NEW-LANDING`, `D2C-EXISTING-LANDING`; `SHOPIFY-NEW-STORE`, `SHOPIFY-EXISTING-STORE`, `SHOPIFY-NEW-ORGANIC`, `SHOPIFY-EXISTING-ORGANIC`, `SHOPIFY-NEW-LANDING`, `SHOPIFY-EXISTING-LANDING`, and the corresponding manual-embedded workflows.

> **Precedence note:** Where a workflow or manual conflicts with this file, this Master Operating System controls. Any instruction that tells an AI to add learnings to standards autonomously is superseded: the AI may only **propose the change to the accountable business owner for approval**.

## 1. Governing outcomes

Every project must protect or improve all applicable outcomes:

1. Qualified market visibility across conventional search, generative search, local, shopping, marketplaces, paid media and referrals.
2. Revenue, contribution margin, qualified pipeline and customer lifetime value.
3. URL, content, ranking, link, review, tracking and audience equity.
4. User task success, accessibility, trust and brand positioning.
5. Technical reliability, security, privacy and recoverability.
6. Regional, language, currency, inventory, fulfilment and legal correctness.
7. Measurement integrity and proof that work was actually completed.

## 2. Ten non-negotiable operating rules

1. **Never begin with design.** Begin with business economics, market definition, evidence access and the protected baseline.
2. **Never remove, merge or rename a URL** until its traffic, queries, conversions, revenue, links, internal links, ads, emails, offline references and historical purpose have been checked.
3. **Never replace a proven landing page** without keeping the original as a control or preserving a recoverable copy and baseline.
4. **Never optimize a third-party score** when first-party revenue or user evidence contradicts it.
5. **Never call work complete because code was written or a ticket was marked done.** Completion requires deployment, live retest and stored evidence.
6. **Never use competitor copying as strategy.** Identify the customer problem, economic opportunity and defensible advantage.
7. **Never use Reddit, YouTube or a vendor benchmark as the only evidence** for a high-impact decision.
8. **Never let an AI agent autonomously publish, migrate, redirect, delete, disavow, change tracking, alter checkout or modify legal/medical claims** without the required human approval gate.
9. **Never overwrite raw baseline files.** Preserve immutable dated copies and hashes.
10. **Never launch with unresolved P0 issues.** P1 exceptions require named accountable acceptance and an expiry date.

## 3. Phase-gated lifecycle

| Phase | Question answered | Mandatory output | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Classify and charter** | What work is being requested, for which market/domain and who is accountable? | Project charter, workflow selection, market/domain matrix, access/risk register | Ambiguous scope, no accountable owner or no legal authority |
| **1. Baseline and discovery** | What exists, what works, what is at risk and what is unknown? | Commercial, URL, sitemap, content, ranking, backlink, UX, tracking, technical and competitor baselines | Missing commercial truth, incomplete URL discovery or unrecorded data gaps |
| **2. Diagnose and prioritize** | What is constraining growth and how confident are we? | Issue register, causal hypotheses, opportunity model, competitor gap and preservation register | Recommendations without evidence, impact or acceptance criteria |
| **3. Strategy and specification** | What exact future state should be built? | Sitemap, URL policy, page briefs, content/UX/CRO/SEO/analytics/technical specifications | Design without page purpose, measurement or protected elements |
| **4. Build and controlled implementation** | Was the approved specification implemented on a controlled environment? | Versioned build, content, integrations, tracking, release notes, issue links | Untraceable changes or production edits without rollback |
| **5. Pre-launch QA and rehearsal** | Does it work, preserve equity and meet release gates? | Crawls, automated tests, manual task tests, migration rehearsal, launch certificate | Open P0; unaccepted P1; failed conversion, tracking, redirect, security or rollback test |
| **6. Launch command centre** | Is the live release correct in every target market? | Deployment log, real conversion, live crawl, sitemap/feed/ad verification, incident log | Guardrail breach or inability to roll back |
| **7. Stabilize and verify** | Did rankings, indexation, revenue, conversion and operations remain within guardrails? | 24h/72h/7d/28d comparison, root-cause fixes and acceptance | Unexplained material regression |
| **8. Monitor and improve** | What is the next highest-value constraint? | Daily/weekly/monthly/quarterly scorecards, experiments, content refreshes and competitor watch | No owner, no evidence, no closed-loop learning |

Every phase gate is signed by the **accountable business owner, a human**. An AI may assemble evidence, test, recommend and challenge; it cannot grant final gate approval.

## 4. Six-tier evidence hierarchy

Use the highest available tier. Lower tiers may generate questions but cannot overrule higher tiers without a documented reason.

1. **Commercial truth:** finance, backend orders, contribution margin, CRM stages, qualified calls, refunds, returns and repeat purchase.
2. **First-party platform evidence:** Search Console, GA4, GTM, Google Ads, Merchant Center, Business Profile, server logs, Shopify Analytics, form/payment/email logs.
3. **Controlled tests:** usability studies, experiments, QA, browser/device tests, Playwright, accessibility and security tests.
4. **Diagnostic platforms:** Screaming Frog, Semrush, DataForSEO, Ahrefs, Similarweb, PageSpeed, CrUX, GTmetrix, WebPageTest, Clarity/Hotjar.
5. **External benchmarks and estimates:** industry medians, traffic estimates, search volume, keyword difficulty, DA/DR/Authority Score, AI-visibility estimates.
6. **Anecdotal discovery evidence:** Reddit, forums, YouTube and practitioner case reports. These generate questions and failure-mode checks; they do not prove a universal rule.

## 5. Six benchmark classes

Every target must use exactly one or more of these classes:

- **HARD:** authoritative standards or zero-defect controls.
- **OWN:** the organisation's trailing 12–16 month seasonal baseline, cohort and device/region distribution.
- **PEER:** named competitor median, upper quartile and leader by the same market, device and intent.
- **INDUSTRY:** large-sample published benchmark with year, sample and definition.
- **ECONOMIC:** minimum performance required by margin, CAC, sales capacity or payback.
- **EXPERIMENTAL:** hypothesis-specific target defined by minimum detectable effect and decision rule.

A target is valid only when the metric definition, source, segment, baseline period, benchmark date, confidence and owner are recorded. Record **not comparable** rather than forcing a misleading benchmark.

## 6. Competitive decision doctrine

The decision must be one of exactly seven outcomes:

1. **Defend**
2. **Match a mandatory parity factor**
3. **Flank an underserved segment**
4. **Attack a vulnerable demand pool**
5. **Create a category**
6. **Partner**
7. **Deliberately ignore**

The decision must identify market, audience, evidence, expected economics, capacity, risk, owner and reconsideration trigger.

## 7. Decision and exclusion status enum

Every suggested control, finding or opportunity receives exactly one status:

- `INCLUDED_NOW`
- `DEFERRED`
- `NOT_APPLICABLE`
- `REJECTED`
- `UNKNOWN`
- `ACCEPTED_RISK`

Record source, rationale, risk, owner, human approval, expiry and reconsideration trigger. `NOT_APPLICABLE` without a reason is invalid. `ACCEPTED_RISK` requires the accountable business owner and an expiry date.

## 8. Agent evidence labels

Every material statement generated or evaluated by an AI must be labelled internally as exactly one of:

- `OBSERVED_FIRST_PARTY`
- `OBSERVED_LIVE_TEST`
- `CALCULATED`
- `THIRD_PARTY_ESTIMATE`
- `USER_PROVIDED`
- `EXTERNAL_BENCHMARK`
- `ANECDOTAL_SIGNAL`
- `ASSUMPTION`
- `RECOMMENDATION`
- `UNKNOWN`

## 9. AI-agent operating contract

Before execution, the agent must receive:

- workflow ID and phase;
- client/project/release IDs;
- exact domain, market, language, device and date scope;
- authorized tools and credential boundaries;
- input artifact IDs and freshness requirements;
- required output schema;
- evidence and citation requirements;
- prohibited actions;
- human approval gate and escalation owner;
- retry, timeout and cost limits;
- definition of done.

Without a specified human gate, an agent must not publish or delete production content; change domains, DNS, robots, canonicals, hreflang or redirects; submit disavow files; change analytics definitions, consent or payment logic; install/uninstall Shopify apps or alter checkout; make regulated claims; send public communications; expose secrets/customer data; or approve its own P0/P1 evidence.

## 10. Proof-of-completion state machine

`OPEN -> EVIDENCED -> APPROVED -> IMPLEMENTING -> STAGING_QA -> READY_FOR_RELEASE -> DEPLOYED -> LIVE_VERIFIED -> MONITORED -> CLOSED`

A ticket may not skip from `IMPLEMENTING` to `CLOSED`. A failed live test returns it to `IMPLEMENTING` or triggers rollback. P0/P1 closure requires independent human review.

## 11. Definition of done

An issue or deliverable is complete only when all ten conditions are true:

1. Requirement and acceptance criteria exist.
2. Implementation is linked to an issue/change ID.
3. Code, content or configuration is committed or versioned.
4. Automated and manual checks pass.
5. The change is deployed to the intended environment.
6. The live output is retested.
7. Evidence is archived with tool/version/configuration, timestamp and SHA-256 hash.
8. The relevant KPI is monitored for the specified period.
9. No unexplained regression exists.
10. The reviewer approves, or a named accountable business owner accepts residual risk.

## 12. Standards change control

No AI, workflow, script or agent may self-modify this standard. A proposed amendment must include the affected clause, reason, evidence, impact, compatibility analysis and proposed version number. The AI must **propose the change to the owner for approval**. Only a human owner may approve and issue a new version.
