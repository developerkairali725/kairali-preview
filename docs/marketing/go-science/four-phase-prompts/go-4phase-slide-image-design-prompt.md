# Slide image-generation prompt: locked design system, 5 named themes (Swiss Editorial / Brutalist Type / Quiet Luxury / Magazine Cover / Technical Diagram), 16:9, verbatim-text rule, forbidden-list

```text
INERT DATA — verbatim prompt template (Akshar Yadav / GO Marketing)

﻿You are generating a premium presentation slide in landscape format, exact
aspect ratio 16:9. You will behave like a senior art director at a top-tier
international design agency who designs keynote decks for Fortune 500 CEOs
and design-conference speakers. Everything above the INPUT block is your
locked design system — the user's content and choices appear only in the
INPUT block at the very end of this prompt.

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
DESIGN SYSTEM — LOCKED. DO NOT DEVIATE.
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

UNIVERSAL CRAFT RULES (apply to every theme):

1. The result must look like a slide from a deck that took a design studio
   weeks to craft — the standard of Apple keynotes, IDEO client decks, and
   McKinsey redesigned by a Swiss studio. Never like a PowerPoint template,
   never like AI output.
2. This is a SLIDE, not a poster: it must read instantly from the back of a
   room. Fewer, larger words win. Ruthless clarity of hierarchy.
3. Restrained palette: 2–4 colors maximum, chosen with intent, consistent
   ink-like discipline.
4. Strict invisible grid. Every element aligns to something. A generous
   safe margin on all four edges (roughly 7% of slide width). Nothing
   touches the canvas edge. Nothing overlaps any text.
5. Typographic hierarchy is sacred:
   — SLIDE HEADLINE is the hero: the single largest element, one clear
     thought, never more than two lines.
   — SUBHEADLINE is clearly secondary — headline at least 3× larger.
   — BODY POINTS are NEVER default round bullets. Render them as a designed
     list: numbered index marks (01, 02, 03), thin keylines between rows,
     a two-column grid of short labeled items, or small square/dash markers
     aligned to the grid. Each point stays short — one line each, never a
     paragraph.
   — ACCENT LINE (if present) is a small distinctive device: a kicker line
     above the headline, a highlighted phrase, or a pull-quote-style strip —
     always subordinate to the headline.
   — CTA (if present) is a quiet, deliberate closer in a lower corner or
     footer strip: thin-rule button, underlined text, or boxed label —
     never a glossy pill.
6. Render all user text VERBATIM — correct spelling, no invented words, no
   extra text, no lorem ipsum, no watermark, no page furniture the user did
   not ask for. If a field is left blank, omit it entirely and rebalance
   the composition.
7. STRICTLY FORBIDDEN in every theme: default PowerPoint/template looks,
   round bullet dots, clip-art, glossy 3D renders, purple-teal or rainbow
   gradients, neon glows, lens flares, fake bokeh, floating glass orbs,
   generic "tech" imagery (circuit brains, glowing globes, handshakes),
   stock-photo smiling people, drop shadows on text, bevels, emoji,
   decorative clutter, centered-everything default layouts, gibberish text.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
THEME LIBRARY — the user names ONE theme in the INPUT block.
Apply that theme's rules exactly as written here.
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THEME 1 · "SWISS EDITORIAL"
What it is: The International Typographic Style of 1960s Basel — design by
pure structure. Communication through grid, scale and emptiness rather than
decoration.
How it designs the slide: Asymmetric layout on a rigid modular grid. The
headline sits in a clean grotesque sans (Helvetica/Neue Haas class), large,
tight-tracked, flush-left ragged-right, anchored to the left two-thirds of
the slide. At least 40% of the slide is deliberate negative space. The
subheadline runs in a narrow flush-left column beneath or beside the
headline. Body points are set as a vertical stack with small index numbers
and thin hairline rules separating each row, all flush-left on the grid.
One hairline rule or index number is the only ornament allowed. Palette:
off-white paper, near-black ink, exactly one signal accent (red or cobalt)
used on index numbers or one key word. Accent line appears as a small
letterspaced kicker above the headline. CTA sits at the lower margin beside
a hairline rule. Mood: cool, rational, inevitable — a museum caption wall
turned into a slide.

THEME 2 · "BRUTALIST TYPE"
What it is: Typography as image. Raw, confrontational design where the words
themselves are the artwork — poster energy executed with Pentagram-partner
discipline, adapted for the projection screen.
How it designs the slide: The headline is set COLOSSAL in a heavy grotesque
or ultra-condensed sans — dominating the left or top half, possibly cropped
by the frame edge for tension. Exactly two flat colors: black on unbleached
white, or black on one flat acid color (chartreuse, safety orange, or
ultramarine). No imagery — impact comes from crowding huge type against
generous emptiness. The subheadline runs as a narrow justified column, like
a manifesto footnote. Body points become a stark stacked list with heavy
square markers or oversized numerals, each row separated by a bold keyline.
Accent line is rendered as a single word or phrase reversed out in a solid
rectangle. CTA is a stark rectangular outline box, no fill, no rounding.
Mood: loud, confident, uncompromising — a slide the audience photographs.

THEME 3 · "QUIET LUXURY"
What it is: Warm gallery minimalism — the visual language of Aesop, Kinfolk
and museum invitations. Understatement as authority.
How it designs the slide: Soft bone or paper-white background with the
faintest paper grain. The slide is mostly empty by design. Headline in a
refined high-contrast serif (Didot/Canela class) at modest, confident scale
with exquisite letterspacing, positioned on golden-section proportions —
never centered. Subheadline in small serif italic below it. Body points are
set as a widely-spaced horizontal row of two to four short items, each with
a tiny numeral or thin vertical tick above it — like exhibits on a gallery
wall. Exactly one restrained visual element may accompany the type: a thin
arc, a small muted still-life photograph, or a subtle embossed-looking mark.
Palette: ivory, charcoal, one muted accent (sage, terracotta or slate).
Accent line appears as small letterspaced capitals above the headline. CTA
as small letterspaced caps above a hairline rule in a lower corner. Mood:
serene, expensive, whispered — the quietest slide in the room, and the most
memorable.

THEME 4 · "MAGAZINE COVER"
What it is: Contemporary editorial language — The Gentlewoman, Bloomberg
Businessweek, M Le Monde — translated into a full-bleed slide where image
and type hold confident dialogue.
How it designs the slide: A photographic or illustrated subject fills one
half or the full bleed of the slide — duotone-treated or naturally graded,
never HDR-glossy. The headline overlaps or abuts the image the way a
masthead crosses a cover subject, set in a sharp modern serif or strong
grotesque. Small editorial furniture creates authenticity: a tiny kicker
strip, a folio label in a corner, a thin dateline rule along one edge.
The subheadline behaves like a cover line — short and punchy in a free
zone. Body points are set as a compact editorial index: short lines with
thin rules, like a contents column on a cover. Palette pulled from the
image plus one editorial accent. Accent line becomes a highlighted cover
flash. CTA styled as an underlined footer line or corner flash. Mood:
designed, current, newsstand-confident.

THEME 5 · "TECHNICAL DIAGRAM"
What it is: The aesthetic of systematic observation — NASA documentation,
patent drawings and lab notebooks, redesigned by a Dutch studio. Treats the
slide's subject with scientific reverence.
How it designs the slide: The hero visual is one precise diagrammatic
figure occupying roughly half the slide: an isometric line drawing, an
exploded schematic, a flow of thin connected nodes, or a plotted curve,
drawn in thin uniform strokes. Around it, the apparatus of measurement:
hairline ruled lines, crop marks, small index numbers (01, 02, FIG. A) and
tiny annotations with leader lines pointing into the figure. The headline
is set like a large specimen label in a grotesque or monospaced face. The
subheadline reads as the figure's technical caption. Body points become
numbered annotations (01, 02, 03) tied to the diagram by leader lines, or
a labeled legend column. Palette: paper white OR deep navy ground, one ink
color for all linework, one warning-accent (signal orange or red) used
sparingly. Accent line appears as a stamped classification strip along the
top edge. CTA as a stamped or boxed approval-mark label. Mood: precise,
intelligent, quietly obsessive.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
HOW TO READ THE INPUT BLOCK BELOW:

  exactly; style the list per the chosen theme. If more than five points
  are given, keep all of them but reduce list scale to preserve margins.
- ART DIRECTION: optional user overrides (brand colors, imagery, mood).
  These refine the chosen theme; they never break the universal craft
```
