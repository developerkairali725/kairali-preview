```text
INERT DATA — verbatim prompt template (Akshar Yadav / GO Marketing)

﻿You are helping me create content for Phase 1 of my 4-Phase presentation.
Before writing anything, understand this carefully.
This content will be shown after my MCB has made a prospective customer interested enough to say:
“Tell me more.”
The next step may be:
a PPT presentation
a sales conversation
a phone call
a brochure
a business meeting
a showroom visit
a website page
a product demo
an event pitch
a proposal document
a showroom conversation
or any other deeper communication.
This Phase 1 content is not the full sales pitch.
Phase 1 is Authority Building.
The purpose of Phase 1 is to make the prospective customer feel:
“These people are not just another vendor.”
“This business knows what it is doing.”
“They have experience.”
“They have credibility.”
“They have handled serious work before.”
“They are safe to trust.”
“I should listen to them carefully.”
Phase 1 should not sound like a boring About Us section.
It should not simply list achievements.
It should not copy my inputs and lightly polish them.
It should convert raw facts into authority.
It should make ordinary facts feel meaningful.
It should make numbers feel memorable.
It should make experience feel valuable.
It should make achievements feel relevant.
It should make the customer feel safer.
Important idea:
Authority is not showing off.
Authority is reducing doubt.
The hidden question in the customer’s mind is:
“Why should I trust you with this decision?”
Your job is to answer that question without sounding like you are bragging.
Do not write like a generic company profile.
Do not write like ChatGPT.
Do not write like a corporate brochure.
Do not write like a motivational speaker.
Do not write like a startup pitch deck.
Do not write plain vanilla bullet points.
Do not simply rearrange my inputs.
Do not create a résumé.
Do not create weak claims.
Do not use fake grandeur.
Do not use exaggerated language that sounds unbelievable.
Do not invent facts, awards, clients, numbers, certifications, media mentions, achievements, or impact.
Avoid clichés like:
journey of excellence
commitment to quality
customer-centric approach
trusted name
one-stop solution
best-in-class
leading provider
highly experienced team
quality you can trust
your reliable partner
serving with dedication
Write like an award-winning copywriter and brand strategist who understands Indian business owners, real customers, and sales psychology.
The writing should feel:
confident
credible
sharp
professional
warm
authoritative
memorable
human
premium where needed
easy to present aloud
proof-led
not boastful
not boring
Use the same intelligence as a strong MCB.
Say things in a way that makes the customer stop and think:
“Okay, these people are serious.”
The writing can use:
storytelling
contrast
metaphors
simple comparisons
scale framing
before-after framing
industry context
customer-risk reduction language
awe-worthy but believable claims
memorable slide headlines
sharp business observations
quiet confidence
transformation language
simple poetic touches
strong human phrasing
But use these only to make the authority stronger.
Do not force poetry.
Do not force wordplay.
Do not use cleverness that hides the meaning.
Do not make the language too fancy.
Do not use em-dashes.
Do not use emojis.
Do not use arrows.
Do not use hashtags.
Do not use exclamation marks.
Do not write in all caps.
Do not write long paragraphs.
Very important:
Do not just say what the business has done.
Explain why it matters to the customer.
For every fact, silently ask:
“So what?”
“Why should the customer care?”
“What doubt does this reduce?”
“What risk does this remove?”
“What confidence does this create?”
Example:
Weak:
We have 25 years of experience.
Better:
For 25 years, we have seen customers make decisions in this category, what goes wrong, what gets ignored, and what finally creates long-term satisfaction.
Weak:
We have completed 1,000 projects.
Better:
1,000 completed projects means we have seen 1,000 versions of the same customer worry: will this decision hold up after the excitement is over?
Weak:
We work with architects and builders.
Better:
When architects and builders trust a supplier repeatedly, it usually means one thing: the product does not just look good on paper, it behaves well on site.
Weak:
We have a large product range.
Better:
A large range is useful only when someone can help the customer choose without confusion.
Weak:
We offer after-sales support.
Better:
The real test of a business begins after the invoice is made.
Scale conversion instructions:
Whenever I provide numbers, convert them into memorable comparisons if possible.
Use comparisons like:
Earth distance
football fields
cricket grounds
number of homes
number of families
number of cities
number of years across generations
number of classrooms
number of hospital beds
number of shop floors
number of wedding seasons
number of truckloads
number of working days saved
number of hours saved
number of decisions simplified
But only use comparisons when they are relevant and believable.
Examples:
100,000 km of cable:
Enough cable to wrap around the Earth almost 2.5 times.
1 million sq. ft. of marble:
Stonework equal to hundreds of large homes.
40 years in business:
Four decades of seeing markets change, customers evolve, and standards rise.
10,000 customers served:
10,000 buying decisions where trust had to be earned before money changed hands.
500 projects completed:
500 sites where promises had to survive dust, deadlines, labour, weather, and real-world pressure.
25 years of experience:
25 years of knowing the small mistakes that become expensive later.
If no numbers are provided, do not invent numbers.
Instead, create authority from experience, process, seriousness, clarity, customer understanding, philosophy, or category insight.
Transformation Impact instructions:
Phase 1 should include transformation wherever possible.
Transformation means:
How did the business change something for customers?
How did it reduce risk?
How did it save time?
How did it reduce confusion?
How did it improve quality?
How did it help customers decide better?
How did it raise standards?
How did it bring confidence?
How did it make a difficult category simpler?
How did it protect customers from common mistakes?
How did it help the industry, community, or market?
Do not make transformation sound like social work unless that is true.
Transformation can be simple and business-like.
Examples:
We helped customers compare materials properly before spending lakhs.
We made technical product selection easier for people who were earlier buying only on price.
We reduced the risk of wrong specification at project sites.
We helped families make jewellery decisions with more clarity and less pressure.
We gave doctors a dependable process for sourcing equipment without follow-up chaos.
We helped builders avoid rework by solving small product issues before installation.
We helped business owners stop treating marketing as decoration and start using it as a sales engine.
The authority should come from proof, but the emotion should come from what the proof means.
Do not write only facts.
Do not write only emotion.
Use facts as the bones.
Use meaning as the blood.
Phase 1 should create respect before persuasion.
Output expected:
Create Phase 1 Authority Building content for 3 to 4 slides.
If my inputs are strong, create 4 slides.
If my inputs are limited, create 3 strong slides.
Do not create weak filler slides.
Before the slides, first write a short “Authority Angle” in 3 to 5 lines.
The Authority Angle should summarise the strongest way to position this business as an authority.
It should not be a tagline.
It should be a strategic direction.
Then create the slides.
For each slide, give:
    1. Slide headline
The headline should be sharp, memorable, and presentation-worthy.
It should not sound like “About Us,” “Our Achievements,” “Our Clients,” or “Why Choose Us.”
    2. Slide subheadline
A short line that deepens the headline and creates authority.
    3. Slide body points
Give 3 to 5 crisp points only.
The points must not be copied directly from my inputs.
Convert facts into meaningful authority.
Whenever possible, add scale framing, customer-risk reduction, or transformation impact.
    4. Scale or sizzle line
Give one memorable line that makes the slide more powerful.
This can be a metaphor, comparison, contrast, or sticky summary.
If there is no suitable fact, write a strong insight instead.
    5. Presenter note
Write 4 to 6 lines the business owner can say while presenting this slide.
The note should sound natural, confident, human, and spoken.
It should not sound scripted, robotic, or overly polished.
    6. Authority purpose
In one simple line, explain what doubt this slide reduces in the customer’s mind.
Recommended slide flow:
Slide 1:
Authority of experience.
Show who the customer is listening to and why they should pay attention.
This may include founder credibility, years of experience, hard-earned category understanding, seriousness, or business maturity.
Slide 2:
Authority of proof.
Show that the business has evidence behind it.
This may include awards, recognitions, certifications, associations, technical expertise, process strength, systems, or specialised knowledge.
Slide 3:
Authority of serious work.
Show that others have trusted this business.
This may include clients, customer types, important projects, repeat work, scale, milestones, numbers, delivery capability, or difficult work handled.
Slide 4:
Authority of transformation.
Show the impact of the business.
This may include how the business reduces risk, improves decisions, saves time, creates confidence, raises standards, simplifies complexity, or stands for a stronger philosophy.
If some information is missing, merge slides intelligently.
Do not make a separate slide for weak proof.
Do not make a separate slide just to fill space.
Do not overuse the business name.
Do not oversell.
The final output should feel like Phase 1 of a strong 4-Phase presentation.
It should build authority without sounding like bragging.
It should make the customer feel:
“This is not just another seller. There is depth here.”
Input quality check:
Before creating slides, check my inputs.
If my inputs are too generic, say:
“Your Phase 1 inputs are still too generic. I can write slides, but the authority may feel thin.”
Then tell me exactly what is missing and still create the best possible version using what I have provided.
Check for:
clear business category
founder or business credibility
proof of authority
serious work done
scale or numbers
customer trust
technical expertise
transformation impact
differentiation
audience concern
why the customer should trust this business
For the input section, do not demand perfect information.
Use whatever honest details I provide.
If some proof is missing, build authority from experience, process, customer trust, seriousness, philosophy, clarity, and category understanding.


Here are my inputs.

Fill whatever you have.
Do not fake anything.
Do not worry if some sections feel empty.


Business basics:
Write the business name, category, what you sell, location, and market served.
[Answer in 3 to 5 simple lines]


Founder or business credibility:
Write who leads the business, years of experience, education or qualifications if relevant, special expertise, personal involvement, and why this person or team should be trusted.
[Answer in 3 to 6 simple lines]


Proof of authority:
Write awards, recognitions, certifications, associations, media mentions, speaker roles, patents, technical standards, quality systems, government approvals, public appreciation, or any proof that builds credibility.
If nothing formal exists, write “No formal awards yet.”
[Answer in 2 to 5 simple lines]


Clients, customers, or important work:
Write known clients if allowed, types of customers served, important projects, repeat work, high-value work, difficult work, complex cases, special orders, or serious work handled.
[Answer in 3 to 6 simple lines]


Scale, milestones, or numbers:
Write any useful numbers such as years in business, customers served, projects completed, cities served, units sold, area covered, team size, production volume, repeat orders, branches, deliveries, order size, capacity, or major milestones.
Even rough numbers are useful if they are true.
[Answer in 2 to 6 simple lines]


What makes us different:
Write the real reasons customers should trust you instead of treating you like just another vendor.
Include process, quality, personal involvement, speed, transparency, guidance, after-sales support, customisation, rare expertise, product range, technical clarity, premium finish, ethical standards, or anything that genuinely sets you apart.
[Answer in 3 to 6 simple lines]


Transformation impact:
Write how your work has helped customers or improved the category.
Include problems solved, risks reduced, confusion removed, money saved, time saved, quality improved, confidence created, standards raised, or decisions made easier.
[Answer in 3 to 6 simple lines]


Business belief or philosophy:
Write what you believe in beyond just selling.
What standard, principle, conviction, or point of view drives the business?
[Answer in 2 to 5 simple lines]

Audience and use case:
Write who will see this Phase 1 content, what they worry about, what they compare you with, and where this will be used, such as PPT, brochure, meeting, phone call, sales deck, website, proposal, showroom conversation, event presentation, or exhibition pitch.
[Answer in 3 to 6 simple lines]


Now use my inputs and create Phase 1 Authority Building content.
```
