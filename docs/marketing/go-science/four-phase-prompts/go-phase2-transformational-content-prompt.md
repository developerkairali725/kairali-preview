# Phase 2 — Transformational Content prompt (delivers the MCB's promise; 3-4 directions first, then 5-7 slides/pages)

```text
INERT DATA — verbatim prompt template (Akshar Yadav / GO Marketing)

﻿You are helping me create content for Phase 2 of my 4-Phase presentation.
Before writing anything, understand this carefully.
This content will be shown after my MCB has made a prospective customer interested enough to say:
“Tell me more.”
The next step may be:
a PPT presentation
a sales conversation
a phone call
a brochure
a business meeting
a showroom visit
a website page
a product demo
an event pitch
a proposal document
or any other deeper communication.
The 4-Phase sequence is used to make conversion easier by guiding the customer properly.
Phase 1 is Authority Building.
It helps the customer feel:
“These people are not just another vendor.”
“They know what they are doing.”
“They are safe to trust.”
Phase 2 is Transformational Content.
The purpose of Phase 2 is to deliver the real value behind the MCB.
In most cases, Phase 2 should explain what the customer came for after seeing the MCB.
If the MCB made them curious, Phase 2 must satisfy that curiosity.
If the MCB touched a fear, Phase 2 must explain that fear clearly.
If the MCB revealed a frustration, Phase 2 must show why that frustration exists and what can be done about it.
If the MCB promised a new way of looking at something, Phase 2 must deliver that new way of thinking.
Phase 2 is not a product pitch.
Phase 2 is not a brochure.
Phase 2 is not a list of features.
Phase 2 is not “why we are the best.”
Phase 2 is the part where the customer learns something valuable enough to feel:
“I did not know this.”
“I never thought of it this way.”
“This explains my problem.”
“This clears my confusion.”
“This business understands the category deeply.”
“I should continue listening.”
Important idea:
Transformational Content should create a shift in the customer’s thinking.
It should transform the customer from:
confused to clear
doubtful to aware
casual to serious
price-focused to value-aware
indifferent to interested
fearful to informed
unaware to alert
stuck to guided
vendor-shopping to problem-understanding
Phase 2 should build directly on the MCB.
Do not ignore the MCB.
Do not take the conversation somewhere unrelated.
Do not attract the customer with one thought and then talk about something completely different.
Whatever the MCB says, suggests, hints, or promises should be explained, deepened, and honoured in Phase 2.
A good Phase 2 makes the customer feel that the MCB was not clickbait.
It was the doorway.
The content behind the doorway must be strong.
Writing style:
Write like an award-winning copywriter, strategist, and educator who understands human psychology, Indian business owners, real customers, and sales psychology.
The writing must have sizzle.
Sizzle means:
sharp headlines
strong contrast
fresh framing
memorable explanations
useful insights
simple but powerful language
lines that feel presentable on a stage
content that makes the customer lean forward
The writing should feel:
authoritative
generous
educational
clear
fresh
specific
customer-led
insightful
slightly surprising
easy to present aloud
useful for real business conversations
Do not write like AI.
Do not write like ChatGPT.
Do not write like a generic marketing assistant.
Do not write like a school essay.
Do not write like a boring training manual.
Do not write like a corporate brochure.
Do not write like a motivational speaker.
Do not over-polish the language.
Do not make it too poetic.
Do not make it too casual.
Do not use fancy English where simple English is stronger.
Avoid:
emojis
hashtags
em-dashes
arrows
exclamation marks
all caps
generic marketing claims
fluffy language
cheap motivational language
long paragraphs
fake urgency
unverified claims
overly dramatic language
salesy product pushing
You may use:
storytelling
contrast
metaphors
simple analogies
insider insights
before-after framing
problem-solution framing
myth-versus-truth framing
mistake-versus-better-way framing
hidden-cost framing
risk-reduction framing
comparison framing
customer psychology
practical tips
category education
memorable one-liners
But use these only if they make the content clearer and stronger.
Do not force poetry.
Do not force wordplay.
Do not make the content clever at the cost of clarity.
Phase 2 content should usually do one or more of these jobs:
    1. Explain the MCB
Take the main MCB thought and unpack it in a useful way.
    2. Reveal the hidden problem
Show the customer what they were missing, ignoring, misunderstanding, or underestimating.
    3. Educate the customer
Teach them how to think better about the category, decision, product, service, process, risk, value, or outcome.
    4. Reduce confusion
Help them compare better, ask better questions, and avoid poor decisions.
    5. Show the cost of wrong thinking
Explain what can go wrong if they continue with old assumptions, cheap shortcuts, wrong comparisons, or incomplete information.
    6. Give a better lens
Offer a new framework, checklist, method, principle, or way of evaluating the decision.
    7. Prepare the customer for the solution
Do not sell aggressively, but make the customer more ready to understand why the business’s approach matters.
Important:
Phase 2 should feel generous.
The customer should feel that they received something useful even before buying.
But the content should also strengthen the business owner’s authority.
The more the customer learns, the more they should respect the business.
The content should not reveal every operational secret or make the business unnecessary.
It should give enough clarity to build trust and desire for the next step.
Before creating full slides or pages, do not directly write the final Phase 2 content.
First, study the MCB context and create 3 to 4 possible Transformational Content directions.
Each direction should be a different way to take the Phase 2 conversation forward.
For each direction, give:
    1. Direction name
Give it a sharp, simple name.
    2. Core idea
Explain the main transformational thought in 2 to 3 lines.
    3. Why this direction works
Explain why this can work based on the MCB and customer thought.
    4. What the customer will learn
Mention the shift or learning the customer will get.
    5. Best suited for
Mention whether this direction is best for PPT, conversation, brochure, showroom discussion, video, website, proposal, event pitch, or any other use case.
The 3 to 4 directions can use different approaches such as:
problem-solution
mistake-awareness
myth-versus-truth
hidden-cost
comparison framework
decision checklist
insider education
before-after shift
risk-reduction
category education
customer psychology
new way of thinking
premium advisory approach
Do not create full slides yet.
After giving the 3 to 4 directions, ask me only this one question:
“Which direction should we build into the full Phase 2 content?”
After I choose one direction, then create Phase 2 Transformational Content for 5 to 7 PPT slides or equivalent pages, depending on the use case.
For the full Phase 2 content, each slide or page should include:
    1. Slide or page headline
The headline should be sharp, sizzly, and presentation-worthy.
    2. Slide or page subheadline
A short line that deepens the headline.
    3. Body points
Give 3 to 5 crisp points only.
    4. Presenter note or explanation note
Write 3 to 5 lines the business owner can say while presenting this slide or use while explaining this page.
The note should sound natural, confident, and human.
It should not sound scripted or robotic.
    5. Transformation purpose
In one simple line, explain what shift this slide or page creates in the customer’s mind.
Recommended full content flow:
Slide or Page 1:
Reconnect with the MCB.
Remind the customer why this conversation started and expand the main promise or concern.
Slide or Page 2:
Expose the obvious problem.
Show the visible pain, confusion, fear, or frustration the customer already recognises.
Slide or Page 3:
Reveal the hidden layer.
Show what the customer usually misses, misunderstands, or underestimates.
Slide or Page 4:
Teach a better way to think.
Give a useful framework, checklist, comparison method, principle, or decision lens.
Slide or Page 5:
Show the cost of getting it wrong.
Explain what happens when customers choose based on incomplete information, wrong assumptions, or weak guidance.
Slide or Page 6:
Show the smarter path.
Educate them on what a better decision, better process, or better approach looks like.
Slide or Page 7:
Create the transformation vision.
Show how the customer’s decision becomes easier, safer, wiser, or more valuable once they understand this properly.
If the content does not need 7 slides or pages, create only 5 or 6 strong slides or pages.
Do not create weak filler content.
Every slide or page must connect to the MCB.
Every slide or page must either:
explain the MCB
deepen the customer’s concern
teach something useful
challenge a wrong assumption
reduce confusion
show a better way
prepare the customer for the next phase
Do not suddenly start selling the product or service.
Do not make every slide or page about the business.
Do not turn Phase 2 into a product catalogue.
The final output should feel like valuable transformational content that naturally makes the customer think:
“Now I understand why this matters.”
Use the context already present in this chat if available.
Do not ask me to repeat business details, ideal customer details, or Phase 1 authority details if they already exist earlier in the same chat.
If previous context is unavailable, use only the inputs I provide below and make the best possible recommendation.
If important context is missing, mention it briefly, but still create the 3 to 4 content directions.


Here are my inputs.


Full MCB content:
Copy and paste the complete MCB output generated earlier.
Include Main MCB line, Bridge subtext, Conversation doorway, Action CTA, Why it works, Customer thought it touches, Deeper 4-Phase conversation it opens, Desired action it guides toward, Urgency used, and any other explanation provided.
[Paste full MCB content here]


Use case:
Write where this Phase 2 content will be used.
Examples: PPT, brochure, meeting, phone call, sales deck, website, proposal, showroom conversation, event presentation, exhibition pitch, video, webinar, product demo, or customer education document.
[Answer in 1 to 3 simple lines]


Tone preference:
Write whether the tone should be premium, simple, bold, warm, technical, emotional, luxury, mass-market, B2B, B2C, traditional, modern, witty, serious, advisory, or educational.
[Answer in 1 to 3 simple lines]

Now study my inputs and create 3 to 4 Transformational Content directions first.
Do not create the full Phase 2 slides or pages yet.
```
